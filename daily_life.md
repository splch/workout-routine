# Muscles Worked by Daily Life

How much an ordinary day works each of the 300 muscles in muscles.json, and which everyday activities do it. Nearly all of them get some work: 44 nonstop, 226 every day and 5 occasionally. Only 25, all vestigial or without a clear job, get barely any.

## What counts as daily life

An ordinary day for a healthy adult who does no exercise: sleep, washing and dressing, meals, conversation, sitting at a desk or table, a few thousand steps, a few flights of stairs, getting in and out of chairs, bed and a car, carrying bags, light chores, phone and keyboard use, and trips to the toilet.

## How to read this list

- Each entry gives how often daily life works the muscle, the activities that work it (most important first) and what the muscle does in them.
- **Nonstop**: Works around the clock, or every waking moment, with no effort from you: the heartbeat, breathing, eye movements and blinking, the airway, digestion, and the tone that keeps the bladder and bowel closed.
- **Every day**: Works many times a day, or for long stretches, in things everyone does: sitting and standing, walking, getting up, eating, talking, facial expression, holding up and turning the head, using the hands.
- **Occasionally**: Works only in some situations, not every day for everyone: cold, arousal, deep labored breathing.
- **Barely**: Vestigial, or no clear everyday action: little or no work in an ordinary day.
- *In N exercises* counts the exercises in calisthenics.json that work the muscle as a primary or secondary mover. Entries without it appear in no exercise.
- *variable* means the muscle is not present in everyone; it is rated for the people who have it.
- Regions, groups and order follow muscles.md.

## Used is not trained

Daily life uses nearly every muscle, but using a muscle is not the same as training it.

- **Worked by living itself.** The heart, the breathing muscles, the muscles of the eyes, face, jaw, tongue and throat, and the sphincters and pelvic floor work all day and much of the night. Few of them appear in any exercise, and daily life covers them: of the 201 muscles that no exercise in calisthenics.json works, it works 176. The other 25 are vestigial or have no clear job. Targeted training still helps some people (the pelvic floor after childbirth, the breathing muscles in lung disease), but ordinary life keeps these muscles working.
- **Real volume.** Standing and walking give steady work to the calves (the soleus above all), the shins, the hip abductors and the small muscles of the foot.
- **The hardest moments.** Rising from a chair and climbing stairs put the heaviest everyday loads on the quadriceps and gluteus maximus. They are also the standard tests of leg strength in older adults, because they are the first things to get hard.
- **Light work only.** The chest, shoulders, arms, lats, upper back and abdominals work every day, but far below what they can do. That keeps them moving, not strong: building strength is the routine's job.

## Head

### Eye: extraocular

- **Levator palpebrae superioris** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking. Holds the upper eyelid open every waking moment; relaxes for each blink and in sleep.
- **Superior rectus** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking. Turns the eye up. The eye muscles work in every glance, about three quick jumps (saccades) a second while awake, and in REM sleep.
- **Inferior rectus** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking. Turns the eye down, as when reading or looking at a phone.
- **Medial rectus** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking. Turns the eye inward; the two work together to converge on anything close, like a screen or a page.
- **Lateral rectus** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking. Turns the eye outward, as when glancing to the side.
- **Superior oblique** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking. Turns the eye down and in and rotates it; needed for looking down while reading or going downstairs.
- **Inferior oblique** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking. Turns the eye up and out and rotates it, keeping the view level when you tilt your head.

### Ear: middle ear

- **Tensor tympani** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking, reflexes. Tightens the eardrum when you chew, swallow and yawn, and in a startle.
- **Stapedius** — *Every day* · talking, reflexes. Damps the smallest ear bone just before and while you speak, and reflexively to loud sounds.

### Facial expression: scalp (epicranius)

- **Occipitofrontalis** — *Every day* · facial expression, looking & blinking. The frontal belly raises the eyebrows in surprise and when looking up; the occipital belly anchors the scalp.
- **Temporoparietalis** — *Barely*. Thin, largely vestigial scalp sheet; little role in an ordinary day.

### Facial expression: eyelids & eyebrows

- **Orbicularis oculi** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking, facial expression. Closes the lids for every blink (about 15,000 a day), squints against glare and crinkles the eyes in a smile; its deep part helps drain tears.
- **Corrugator supercilii** — *Every day* · facial expression, looking & blinking. Draws the brows together to frown, concentrate or squint against glare.
- **Depressor supercilii** — *Every day* · facial expression, looking & blinking. Pulls the brow down when frowning or shading the eyes from light.

### Facial expression: nose

- **Procerus** — *Every day* · facial expression. Pulls the skin between the brows down, wrinkling the bridge of the nose in a frown or squint.
- **Nasalis** — *Every day* · breathing, facial expression. Flares the nostrils when you sniff or breathe hard, and compresses them.
- **Depressor septi nasi** — *Every day* · facial expression, breathing. Pulls the tip of the nose down when you smile or talk and helps widen the nostrils on a deep breath.

### Facial expression: mouth & cheeks

- **Orbicularis oris** — *Every day* · talking, eating & swallowing, facial expression. Closes and purses the lips for speech (p, b, m, w), sipping and keeping food and drink in the mouth.
- **Buccinator** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Holds the cheeks against the teeth to keep food between them while chewing; also used to suck and blow.
- **Levator labii superioris** — *Every day* · facial expression, talking. Raises the upper lip in a broad smile, a sneer or speech.
- **Levator labii superioris alaeque nasi** — *Every day* · facial expression. Lifts the upper lip and flares the nostril, as in a sneer or a broad smile.
- **Levator anguli oris** — *Every day* · facial expression. Lifts the corners of the mouth in a smile.
- **Zygomaticus major** — *Every day* · facial expression, talking. The main smiling muscle: draws the corners of the mouth up and out.
- **Zygomaticus minor** — *Every day* · facial expression. Lifts the upper lip to show the upper teeth when smiling.
- **Risorius** *(variable)* — *Every day* · facial expression. Where present, pulls the corners of the mouth sideways in a grin.
- **Depressor anguli oris** — *Every day* · facial expression, talking. Pulls the corners of the mouth down, as in a frown or grimace.
- **Transversus menti** *(variable)* — *Every day* · facial expression. Where present, works with depressor anguli oris.
- **Depressor labii inferioris** — *Every day* · facial expression, talking. Lowers the lower lip in speech, pouting and showing the lower teeth.
- **Mentalis** — *Every day* · facial expression, eating & swallowing. Raises and pushes out the lower lip, as in pouting or sealing the lip against a cup, and wrinkles the chin.

### Facial expression: ear (extrinsic auricular)

- **Anterior auricular** — *Barely*. Vestigial; pulls the ear forward only in people who can wiggle their ears.
- **Superior auricular** — *Barely*. Vestigial; lifts the ear only in people who can wiggle their ears.
- **Posterior auricular** — *Barely* · reflexes. Vestigial; twitches reflexively to sudden sounds, too little to move the ear in most people.

### Ear: intrinsic auricular

- **Helicis minor** — *Barely*. Vestigial ear-shape muscle; no useful action in humans.
- **Tragicus** — *Barely*. Vestigial ear-shape muscle; no useful action in humans.
- **Pyramidal muscle of auricle** *(variable)* — *Barely*. Vestigial ear-shape muscle; no useful action in humans.
- **Antitragicus** — *Barely*. Vestigial ear-shape muscle; no useful action in humans.
- **Transverse muscle of auricle** — *Barely*. Vestigial ear-shape muscle; no useful action in humans.
- **Oblique muscle of auricle** — *Barely*. Vestigial ear-shape muscle; no useful action in humans.
- **Muscle of terminal notch** *(variable)* — *Barely*. Vestigial ear-shape muscle; no useful action in humans.

### Mastication

- **Masseter** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing. The main chewing muscle: closes the jaw to bite and chew, and clenches the teeth.
- **Temporalis** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Closes and pulls back the jaw in chewing, and helps hold it closed at rest.
- **Lateral pterygoid** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Opens the jaw and moves it forward and side to side, in chewing, talking and yawning.
- **Medial pterygoid** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing. Closes the jaw with the masseter and grinds food side to side.
- **Pterygoideus proprius** *(variable)* — *Every day* · eating & swallowing. Where present, works with the lateral pterygoid.

### Tongue: extrinsic

- **Genioglossus** — *Nonstop* · breathing, eating & swallowing, talking. Pushes the tongue forward; its activity with each breath keeps the airway open, awake and asleep, and it shapes every swallow and word.
- **Hyoglossus** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Pulls the sides of the tongue down in chewing, swallowing and speech.
- **Chondroglossus** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Part of hyoglossus; works with it.
- **Triticeoglossus** *(variable)* — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Where present, pulls the tongue back and down with hyoglossus.
- **Styloglossus** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Pulls the tongue up and back to start each swallow and to make sounds like k and g.
- **Palatoglossus** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Raises the back of the tongue and narrows the entrance to the throat in swallowing.

### Tongue: intrinsic

- **Superior longitudinal muscle of tongue** — *Every day* · talking, eating & swallowing. Curls the tongue tip up for sounds like t, d and l, and moves food around the mouth.
- **Inferior longitudinal muscle of tongue** — *Every day* · talking, eating & swallowing. Curls the tongue tip down in speech and swallowing.
- **Transverse muscle of tongue** — *Every day* · talking, eating & swallowing. Narrows and lengthens the tongue, as for s sounds or sticking it out.
- **Vertical muscle of tongue** — *Every day* · talking, eating & swallowing. Flattens and widens the tongue in chewing and speech.

### Soft palate

- **Levator veli palatini** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Lifts the soft palate to seal off the nose in every swallow and most speech sounds.
- **Tensor veli palatini** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing. Tenses the soft palate and opens the Eustachian tube when you swallow or yawn, equalizing ear pressure.
- **Musculus uvulae** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Shortens and thickens the uvula to help seal off the nose.
- **Palatopharyngeus** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing. Raises the throat and closes off the nasal passage during swallowing.

## Neck

### Superficial & lateral

- **Platysma** — *Every day* · facial expression. Tenses the skin of the neck and pulls the mouth corners down in a grimace or when bracing.
- **Sternocleidomastoid** — *Every day* · holding & turning the head, breathing. Turns and tilts the head and lifts it off the pillow; lifts the breastbone in hard breathing. *In 7 exercises.*

### Suprahyoid

- **Digastric** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Opens the jaw, hardest in a yawn, and raises the hyoid bone in each swallow.
- **Stylohyoid** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing. Pulls the hyoid bone up and back when you swallow.
- **Mylohyoid** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Raises the floor of the mouth, pushing the tongue up in each swallow.
- **Geniohyoid** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing. Pulls the hyoid bone forward in swallowing, widening the throat, and helps open the jaw.

### Infrahyoid (strap muscles)

- **Sternohyoid** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Pulls the hyoid bone back down after each swallow and steadies it in speech.
- **Omohyoid** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Pulls the hyoid bone down after swallowing and steadies it in speech.
- **Sternothyroid** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Pulls the larynx down after swallowing and for low notes.
- **Thyrohyoid** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Raises the larynx toward the hyoid bone in swallowing and speech.
- **Levator glandulae thyroideae** *(variable)* — *Barely*. Inconstant band on the thyroid gland; no clear action.

### Prevertebral

- **Longus colli** — *Every day* · holding & turning the head. Steadies the curve of the neck and bends it forward; works whenever you hold the head up or nod. *In 7 exercises.*
- **Longus capitis** — *Every day* · holding & turning the head. Nods the head forward and steadies it on the neck. *In 7 exercises.*
- **Rectus capitis anterior** — *Every day* · holding & turning the head. Nods the head at the skull joint and steadies it.
- **Rectus capitis lateralis** — *Every day* · holding & turning the head. Tilts the head sideways and steadies it on the top vertebra.

### Scalenes

- **Anterior scalene** — *Nonstop* · breathing, holding & turning the head. Lifts the first rib with every breath, quiet ones included, and bends the neck sideways. *In 8 exercises.*
- **Middle scalene** — *Nonstop* · breathing, holding & turning the head. Lifts the first rib with every breath and bends the neck sideways. *In 8 exercises.*
- **Posterior scalene** — *Nonstop* · breathing, holding & turning the head. Lifts the second rib with every breath and bends the neck sideways. *In 8 exercises.*
- **Scalenus minimus** *(variable)* — *Barely*. Where present, tightens the membrane over the top of the lung; no clear everyday action.

### Suboccipital

- **Rectus capitis posterior major** — *Every day* · holding & turning the head, looking & blinking. Tips the head back and turns it; fine-tunes head position as the eyes move.
- **Rectus capitis posterior minor** — *Every day* · holding & turning the head, looking & blinking. Tips the head back and steadies it; unusually rich in stretch sensors that track head position.
- **Obliquus capitis superior** — *Every day* · holding & turning the head, looking & blinking. Tips the head back and sideways in small posture adjustments.
- **Obliquus capitis inferior** — *Every day* · holding & turning the head, looking & blinking. Turns the head at the top two vertebrae, as in looking over your shoulder.

### Pharynx

- **Superior pharyngeal constrictor** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing. Squeezes food, drink and saliva down the throat in each swallow, several hundred times a day.
- **Middle pharyngeal constrictor** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing. Squeezes each swallow down the throat.
- **Inferior pharyngeal constrictor** — *Nonstop* · eating & swallowing, digestion. Squeezes each swallow down; its lowest part (cricopharyngeus) stays clenched day and night as the upper esophageal sphincter, opening only to let a swallow through.
- **Stylopharyngeus** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing. Lifts and widens the throat to take each swallow.
- **Salpingopharyngeus** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing. Raises the throat in swallowing and helps open the Eustachian tube.

### Larynx

- **Cricothyroid** — *Every day* · talking. Stretches the vocal folds to raise the pitch of the voice.
- **Posterior cricoarytenoid** — *Nonstop* · breathing, talking. The only muscle that opens the vocal folds; widens them a little with every breath in, awake and asleep.
- **Ceratocricoid** *(variable)* — *Barely*. Inconstant slip beside the posterior cricoarytenoid; its action, if any, is unclear.
- **Lateral cricoarytenoid** — *Every day* · talking, coughing & straining. Closes the vocal folds to make voice, and to hold the breath before a cough or a lift.
- **Thyroarytenoid** — *Every day* · talking, eating & swallowing, coughing & straining. Shortens and closes the vocal folds for speech, and seals the airway in swallowing and coughing.
- **Vocalis** — *Every day* · talking. Fine-tunes the tension of the vocal folds for pitch and voice quality.
- **Oblique arytenoid** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, talking. Closes the entrance to the larynx in each swallow and helps close the vocal folds.
- **Transverse arytenoid** — *Every day* · talking, eating & swallowing. Closes the back of the vocal folds for voice and in swallowing.

## Back

### Superficial (move the shoulder girdle)

- **Trapezius** — *Every day* · lifting & carrying, reaching & dressing, holding & turning the head. The upper part holds up the shoulders against the weight of the arms and anything carried; the whole muscle turns the shoulder blade up when you reach overhead. *In 181 exercises.*
- **Latissimus dorsi** — *Every day* · getting up & down, pushing & pulling, coughing & straining. Pulls the arm down and back: pushing up from armrests or a bed, pulling a door, reaching into a back pocket; also squeezes the chest in a hard cough. *In 150 exercises.*
- **Rhomboid major** — *Every day* · pushing & pulling, lifting & carrying, reaching & dressing. Pulls the shoulder blade back and steadies it when you pull, carry or reach. *In 70 exercises.*
- **Rhomboid minor** — *Every day* · pushing & pulling, lifting & carrying, reaching & dressing. Pulls the shoulder blade back and steadies it, with the major. *In 70 exercises.*
- **Levator scapulae** — *Every day* · holding & turning the head, lifting & carrying. Lifts the shoulder blade and bends the neck; works in shrugging, carrying and holding the head forward over a screen. *In 6 exercises.*
- **Transversus nuchae** *(variable)* — *Barely*. Inconstant slip over the top of the back of the neck; no clear action.

## Thorax

### Pectoral

- **Pectoralis major** — *Every day* · pushing & pulling, lifting & carrying, reaching & dressing, getting up & down. Brings the arm forward and across: pushing doors, carrying things in front of you, hugging, pushing up from a chair. *In 176 exercises.*
- **Pectoralis minor** — *Every day* · reaching & dressing, pushing & pulling, breathing. Draws the shoulder blade forward and down when you reach or push; helps lift the ribs in deep breaths. *In 2 exercises.*
- **Subclavius** — *Every day* · reaching & dressing, lifting & carrying. Steadies the collarbone against the breastbone as the arm moves.
- **Serratus anterior** — *Every day* · reaching & dressing, pushing & pulling. Holds the shoulder blade to the ribs and turns it up when you reach forward or overhead or push. *In 201 exercises.*
- **Sternalis** *(variable)* — *Barely*. Present in a small minority; no known action.

### Intercostal & costal

- **External intercostals** — *Nonstop* · breathing. Lift the ribs with every breath in and keep the chest wall firm.
- **Internal intercostals** — *Nonstop* · breathing, coughing & straining, talking. The front parts help lift the ribs with every breath in; the side parts pull the ribs down in speech, coughing and forced breathing out.
- **Innermost intercostals** — *Every day* · coughing & straining, talking. Work with the internal intercostals, pulling the ribs down in speech, coughs and forced breaths out.
- **Subcostales** — *Every day* · coughing & straining. Thought to pull the lower ribs down with the internal intercostals.
- **Transversus thoracis** — *Every day* · coughing & straining, talking. Pulls the front of the ribs down when breathing out takes effort: speaking, coughing, laughing.
- **Levatores costarum** — *Every day* · breathing, sitting & standing. Lift the ribs when you breathe in and help bend and turn the spine.

### Diaphragm

- **Diaphragm** — *Nonstop* · breathing, coughing & straining, bladder & bowel. The main breathing muscle: contracts with every breath, roughly 20,000 a day, awake and asleep. Also pushes down to cough, sneeze, strain on the toilet and brace for a lift. *In 2 exercises.*

## Abdomen

### Anterolateral wall

- **Rectus abdominis** — *Every day* · getting up & down, coughing & straining, bending & picking up. Curls the trunk to sit up in bed or from a sofa, and squeezes the belly in coughs, laughs and straining. *In 586 exercises.*
- **Pyramidalis** *(variable)* — *Barely*. Where present, tenses the midline of the belly wall; no clear everyday role.
- **External oblique** — *Every day* · coughing & straining, sitting & standing, getting up & down. Turns and side-bends the trunk, steadies it when you reach or carry, and squeezes the belly to cough, laugh or strain. *In 580 exercises.*
- **Internal oblique** — *Every day* · coughing & straining, sitting & standing, getting up & down. Turns and side-bends the trunk with the external oblique; its lower fibers stay lightly active while you stand. *In 580 exercises.*
- **Transversus abdominis** — *Every day* · coughing & straining, sitting & standing, lifting & carrying, bladder & bowel. Tightens the belly like a corset: switches on just before you move a limb, and in coughs, laughs, lifts and bearing down on the toilet. *In 539 exercises.*
- **Cremaster** — *Occasionally* · reflexes, sexual function. Lifts the testis in cold, when the inner thigh is touched (the cremasteric reflex) and in arousal; in females only a few fibers.

### Posterior wall

- **Quadratus lumborum** — *Every day* · walking, lifting & carrying, sitting & standing. Hikes the hip and holds the trunk level when you walk or carry a bag in one hand; anchors the lowest rib for breathing. *In 17 exercises.*

## Pelvis & perineum

### Pelvic floor: levator ani

- **Pubococcygeus** — *Nonstop* · bladder & bowel, sitting & standing, coughing & straining. Levator ani keeps a steady tone all day to hold up the pelvic organs, and clenches reflexively when you cough, sneeze, laugh or lift. *In 1 exercise.*
- **Puboperinealis** — *Nonstop* · bladder & bowel. Part of levator ani; supports the perineal body with the rest of the pelvic floor.
- **Pubovaginalis / Puboprostaticus** — *Nonstop* · bladder & bowel. Part of levator ani; supports the vagina or prostate and helps close the urethra.
- **Puboanalis** — *Nonstop* · bladder & bowel. Part of levator ani; supports the anal canal.
- **Puborectalis** — *Nonstop* · bladder & bowel. Sling around the rectum that stays contracted to keep the bowel closed; relaxes only when you empty it. *In 1 exercise.*
- **Iliococcygeus** — *Nonstop* · bladder & bowel, sitting & standing. Part of levator ani; forms the back of the pelvic floor and holds up the pelvic organs. *In 1 exercise.*

### Pelvic floor: coccygeus

- **Coccygeus** — *Every day* · bladder & bowel, sitting & standing. Supports the back of the pelvic floor and pulls the tailbone forward again after a bowel movement. *In 1 exercise.*

### Anal triangle

- **External anal sphincter** — *Nonstop* · bladder & bowel, coughing & straining. Keeps a steady clench day and night, tightens when you cough or hold in gas, and relaxes to let you empty the bowel.

### Urogenital triangle: superficial pouch

- **Superficial transverse perineal** — *Every day* · bladder & bowel, coughing & straining. Steadies the perineal body, the pelvic floor's central anchor, when you cough, lift or strain.
- **Ischiocavernosus** — *Occasionally* · sexual function. Stiffens the erect penis or clitoris; works mainly in sexual activity.
- **Bulbospongiosus** — *Every day* · bladder & bowel, sexual function. In males, clears the last urine from the urethra after each void and expels semen; in females, narrows the vaginal opening.

### Urogenital triangle: deep pouch

- **Deep transverse perineal** — *Every day* · bladder & bowel. Steadies the perineal body and supports the urethral sphincter.
- **External urethral sphincter** — *Nonstop* · bladder & bowel, coughing & straining. Keeps the urethra closed around the clock and squeezes harder when you cough, laugh or need to hold on; relaxes to let you urinate.
- **Compressor urethrae** — *Nonstop* · bladder & bowel. Female; part of the urethral sphincter complex that keeps the urethra closed.
- **Sphincter urethrovaginalis** — *Nonstop* · bladder & bowel. Female; wraps the urethra and vagina and helps keep the urethra closed.

## Upper limb

### Shoulder

- **Deltoid** — *Every day* · reaching & dressing, lifting & carrying. Lifts the arm in every direction: reaching for a shelf, washing your hair, holding a bag away from the body. *In 375 exercises.*
- **Supraspinatus** — *Every day* · reaching & dressing, lifting & carrying. Starts every arm raise and keeps the shoulder joint seated, even while a bag hangs from your hand. *In 3 exercises.*
- **Infraspinatus** — *Every day* · reaching & dressing. Turns the arm out and steadies the shoulder: brushing your hair, reaching to the side. *In 16 exercises.*
- **Teres minor** — *Every day* · reaching & dressing. Turns the arm out and steadies the shoulder with infraspinatus. *In 16 exercises.*
- **Subscapularis** — *Every day* · reaching & dressing, pushing & pulling. Turns the arm in and guards the front of the shoulder: tucking in a shirt, reaching behind your back, pushing. *In 3 exercises.*
- **Teres major** — *Every day* · getting up & down, pushing & pulling. Pulls the arm down and back with the latissimus dorsi, as in pushing up from armrests or pulling a door. *In 91 exercises.*

### Arm: anterior (flexor) compartment

- **Biceps brachii** — *Every day* · eating & swallowing, lifting & carrying, gripping. Bends the elbow and turns the palm up: bringing a fork or cup to your mouth, carrying bags on the forearm, turning a key. *In 125 exercises.*
- **Coracobrachialis** — *Every day* · reaching & dressing. Brings the arm forward and across the body.
- **Brachialis** — *Every day* · lifting & carrying, eating & swallowing. The main elbow bender whatever the palm's position; works whenever you lift something toward you. *In 49 exercises.*

### Arm: posterior (extensor) compartment

- **Triceps brachii** — *Every day* · getting up & down, pushing & pulling. Straightens the elbow: pushing up from a chair or bed, pushing doors and carts. *In 285 exercises.*
- **Anconeus** — *Every day* · pushing & pulling. Helps straighten the elbow and steadies it as the forearm turns. *In 2 exercises.*
- **Articularis cubiti** — *Every day* · pushing & pulling. Draws the elbow capsule clear each time the arm straightens.

### Forearm: anterior, superficial

- **Pronator teres** — *Every day* · fine hand work, gripping. Turns the palm down, for typing or pouring, and helps bend the elbow. *In 1 exercise.*
- **Flexor carpi radialis** — *Every day* · gripping, fine hand work. Bends the wrist and steadies it in grip. *In 154 exercises.*
- **Palmaris longus** *(variable)* — *Every day* · gripping. Where present, tenses the palm and weakly bends the wrist in grip.
- **Flexor carpi ulnaris** — *Every day* · gripping, lifting & carrying, fine hand work. Bends the wrist toward the little finger and steadies it when you carry, hammer or type. *In 154 exercises.*

### Forearm: anterior, intermediate

- **Flexor digitorum superficialis** — *Every day* · gripping, lifting & carrying, fine hand work. Bends the fingers in every grip: bags, cups, rails, phones. *In 154 exercises.*

### Forearm: anterior, deep

- **Flexor digitorum profundus** — *Every day* · gripping, lifting & carrying, fine hand work. The only muscle that bends the fingertips; takes the load in hook grips such as carrying shopping bags. *In 154 exercises.*
- **Flexor pollicis longus** — *Every day* · gripping, fine hand work. Bends the thumb tip for pinching, writing and texting. *In 154 exercises.*
- **Pronator quadratus** — *Every day* · fine hand work, gripping. The main palm-down turner; also holds the forearm bones together when you grip or push.

### Forearm: posterior, superficial

- **Brachioradialis** — *Every day* · lifting & carrying, eating & swallowing, gripping. Bends the elbow with the thumb up, as when lifting a mug or carrying a bag by its handle. *In 8 exercises.*
- **Extensor carpi radialis longus** — *Every day* · gripping, fine hand work. Holds the wrist cocked back during grip and typing. *In 4 exercises.*
- **Extensor carpi radialis brevis** — *Every day* · gripping, fine hand work. The main wrist stabilizer in grip and typing; the muscle usually behind tennis elbow. *In 4 exercises.*
- **Extensor digitorum** — *Every day* · fine hand work, gripping. Straightens the fingers to let go of things, type and spread the hand. *In 4 exercises.*
- **Extensor digiti minimi** — *Every day* · fine hand work. Straightens the little finger.
- **Extensor carpi ulnaris** — *Every day* · gripping, fine hand work. Steadies the wrist in grip and bends it toward the little finger, as when steering a mouse. *In 4 exercises.*

### Forearm: posterior, deep

- **Supinator** — *Every day* · gripping, fine hand work. Turns the palm up: turning a doorknob or key, using a screwdriver, holding out a hand. *In 1 exercise.*
- **Abductor pollicis longus** — *Every day* · gripping, fine hand work. Swings the thumb out from the palm to open the hand around objects.
- **Extensor pollicis brevis** — *Every day* · fine hand work, gripping. Straightens the base of the thumb, in texting and in opening the hand to grip.
- **Extensor pollicis longus** — *Every day* · fine hand work. Straightens the thumb tip and pulls it back, lifting it off a phone screen or space bar.
- **Extensor indicis** — *Every day* · fine hand work. Straightens the index finger to point, tap a screen or click.

### Hand: thenar & adductor

- **Abductor pollicis brevis** — *Every day* · gripping, fine hand work. Moves the thumb away from the palm to wrap around a cup or phone.
- **Flexor pollicis brevis** — *Every day* · gripping, fine hand work. Bends the thumb at its base in pinch and grip.
- **Opponens pollicis** — *Every day* · gripping, fine hand work. Swings the thumb across the palm to meet the fingertips, the basis of every pinch and grasp.
- **Adductor pollicis** — *Every day* · gripping, fine hand work. Presses the thumb against the hand, as in holding keys, a card or a phone.

### Hand: hypothenar

- **Palmaris brevis** — *Every day* · gripping. Wrinkles and cushions the skin at the little-finger edge of the palm to firm up a grip.
- **Abductor digiti minimi (hand)** — *Every day* · gripping, fine hand work. Spreads the little finger for a wide grip or a reach across the keyboard.
- **Flexor digiti minimi brevis (hand)** — *Every day* · gripping. Bends the little finger at its base.
- **Opponens digiti minimi (hand)** — *Every day* · gripping. Cups the palm by rolling the little finger toward the thumb.

### Hand: lumbricals & interossei

- **Lumbricals (hand)** — *Every day* · fine hand work, gripping. Bend the knuckles while straightening the fingers: holding a card, typing, writing. *In 11 exercises.*
- **Dorsal interossei (hand)** — *Every day* · fine hand work, gripping. Spread the fingers and steady them in grip and typing. *In 11 exercises.*
- **Palmar interossei** — *Every day* · fine hand work, gripping. Draw the fingers together, as when holding a flat hand or a sheet of paper. *In 11 exercises.*
- **Pollical palmar interosseus** *(variable)* — *Every day* · fine hand work, gripping. Where recognized, draws the thumb in with adductor pollicis.

## Lower limb

### Hip: iliopsoas

- **Psoas major** — *Every day* · walking, stairs & slopes, sitting & standing. Lifts the thigh in every step and stair and steadies the lower spine when you sit upright. *In 186 exercises.*
- **Psoas minor** *(variable)* — *Barely*. Absent in many people; where present, a weak helper in bending the lower trunk with no clear everyday job.
- **Iliacus** — *Every day* · walking, stairs & slopes, sitting & standing. Swings the leg forward in every step with psoas major and tilts the pelvis to sit upright. *In 186 exercises.*

### Gluteal

- **Gluteus maximus** — *Every day* · getting up & down, stairs & slopes, bending & picking up, walking. Straightens the hip to stand up, climb stairs and come up from a bend; level walking uses little of it. *In 269 exercises.*
- **Gluteus medius** — *Every day* · walking, sitting & standing, stairs & slopes. Keeps the pelvis level each time a foot leaves the ground, in every step and stair. *In 65 exercises.*
- **Gluteus minimus** — *Every day* · walking, sitting & standing. Keeps the pelvis level and the hip steady with gluteus medius. *In 65 exercises.*
- **Tensor fasciae latae** — *Every day* · walking, sitting & standing. Steadies the hip and knee through the iliotibial band in walking and standing. *In 3 exercises.*

### Hip: deep lateral rotators

- **Piriformis** — *Every day* · walking, sitting & standing. Turns the thigh out and steadies the hip joint in walking and standing. *In 13 exercises.*
- **Superior gemellus** — *Every day* · walking, sitting & standing. Steadies the hip joint and turns the thigh out with the other deep rotators. *In 13 exercises.*
- **Obturator internus** — *Every day* · walking, sitting & standing. Steadies the hip joint; part of the pelvic floor is anchored to its covering. *In 13 exercises.*
- **Inferior gemellus** — *Every day* · walking, sitting & standing. Steadies the hip joint and turns the thigh out. *In 13 exercises.*
- **Quadratus femoris** — *Every day* · walking, sitting & standing. Turns the thigh out and steadies the hip joint. *In 13 exercises.*
- **Obturator externus** — *Every day* · walking, sitting & standing. Turns the thigh out and steadies the hip joint. *In 13 exercises.*

### Thigh: anterior (quadriceps & sartorius)

- **Sartorius** — *Every day* · walking, getting up & down. Bends and turns out the thigh and bends the knee: crossing your legs, stepping into a car, sitting cross-legged.
- **Rectus femoris** — *Every day* · walking, stairs & slopes, getting up & down. Straightens the knee and lifts the thigh: swinging each step forward, climbing stairs, standing up. *In 294 exercises.*
- **Vastus lateralis** — *Every day* · getting up & down, stairs & slopes, walking. Straightens the knee to stand up and climb, and cushions each step and each stair on the way down. *In 166 exercises.*
- **Vastus intermedius** — *Every day* · getting up & down, stairs & slopes, walking. Straightens the knee with the other vasti. *In 166 exercises.*
- **Vastus medialis** — *Every day* · getting up & down, stairs & slopes, walking. Straightens the knee and steadies the kneecap, especially as the leg straightens fully. *In 166 exercises.*
- **Articularis genus** — *Every day* · walking, getting up & down. Pulls the knee capsule up out of the way whenever the knee straightens.

### Thigh: medial (adductors)

- **Pectineus** — *Every day* · walking. Draws the thigh forward and in during each step. *In 76 exercises.*
- **Adductor longus** — *Every day* · walking, sitting & standing. Pulls the leg in and steadies the pelvis in walking and standing. *In 76 exercises.*
- **Adductor brevis** — *Every day* · walking. Pulls the leg in and helps bring it forward in each step. *In 76 exercises.*
- **Adductor magnus** — *Every day* · walking, getting up & down, stairs & slopes. Its hamstring part straightens the hip to stand up and climb; its adductor part steadies the leg in each step. *In 76 exercises.*
- **Adductor minimus** — *Every day* · walking. Upper part of adductor magnus; works with it.
- **Gracilis** — *Every day* · walking. Pulls the leg in, helps bend the knee and steadies the inner knee in each step. *In 76 exercises.*

### Thigh: posterior (hamstrings)

- **Biceps femoris** — *Every day* · walking, bending & picking up, getting up & down. Brakes the swinging leg at the end of each step and helps straighten the hip when you rise from a bend or a chair. *In 135 exercises.*
- **Semitendinosus** — *Every day* · walking, bending & picking up, getting up & down. Brakes the swinging leg and helps straighten the hip, like biceps femoris; also steadies the inner knee. *In 135 exercises.*
- **Semimembranosus** — *Every day* · walking, bending & picking up, getting up & down. Brakes the leg at the end of each step and helps straighten the hip. *In 135 exercises.*

### Leg: anterior

- **Tibialis anterior** — *Every day* · walking, sitting & standing. Lifts the foot in every step so the toes clear the ground, lowers it gently after heel strike, and corrects your balance when standing. *In 5 exercises.*

### Leg: posterior, superficial (calf)

- **Plantaris** *(variable)* — *Every day* · walking. Where present, a thin helper of the calf.

## Smooth & cardiac

### Eye (smooth)

- **Ciliary muscle** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking. Focuses the eye: contracts for as long as you look at anything close, like a screen, book or phone, and relaxes for distance.
- **Sphincter pupillae** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking, reflexes. Narrows the pupil in bright light and for near vision, adjusting all day.
- **Dilator pupillae** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking, reflexes. Widens the pupil in dim light and with alertness or excitement.
- **Superior tarsal** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking. Its steady tone lifts the upper lid an extra millimeter or two while you are awake; the lid droops when you are tired.
- **Inferior tarsal** — *Nonstop* · looking & blinking. Its steady tone holds the lower lid down slightly.
- **Orbitalis** — *Barely*. Vestigial in humans; may push the eye slightly forward.

### Skin (smooth)

- **Arrector pili** — *Occasionally* · reflexes. Raises the hairs into goose bumps in cold, fear or a thrill; one per hair follicle.
- **Dartos** — *Every day* · reflexes. Wrinkles and tightens the scrotal skin in cold and relaxes it in warmth, adjusting testicular temperature through the day.
- **Corrugator cutis ani** — *Barely*. Wrinkles the skin around the anus; no meaningful everyday job.

### Airway (smooth)

- **Trachealis** — *Nonstop* · breathing, coughing & straining. Keeps tone in the back wall of the windpipe and narrows it in a cough to speed the airflow.

### Digestive tract (smooth)

- **Bronchoesophageus** — *Barely*. Anchoring band between the esophagus and the left bronchus; no clear active role.
- **Pleuroesophageus** — *Barely*. Anchoring band between the esophagus and the pleura; no clear active role.
- **Pyloric sphincter** — *Nonstop* · digestion. Lets food out of the stomach a little at a time for hours after each meal, and closes against backflow from the gut.
- **Suspensory muscle of duodenum** — *Every day* · digestion. Thought to widen the bend where the duodenum meets the jejunum, easing food along.
- **Sphincter of hepatopancreatic ampulla** — *Nonstop* · digestion. Holds back bile and pancreatic juice between meals and relaxes after you eat to release them.
- **Sphincter of bile duct** — *Nonstop* · digestion. Keeps the bile duct shut between meals so bile fills the gallbladder.
- **Sphincter of pancreatic duct** — *Nonstop* · digestion. Keeps bile from backing up into the pancreatic duct.
- **Internal anal sphincter** — *Nonstop* · bladder & bowel. Provides most of the resting clench that keeps the anus closed day and night; relaxes briefly as the rectum fills so its contents can be sensed.
- **Rectococcygeus** — *Every day* · bladder & bowel. Anchors the rectum to the tailbone; thought to steady it during a bowel movement.
- **Rectourethralis** — *Barely*. Male; a band tying the rectum to the urethra, important in surgery but with no clear active role.

### Urinary tract (smooth)

- **Detrusor** — *Every day* · bladder & bowel. Relaxes as the bladder fills, then squeezes it empty each time you urinate, typically four to eight times a day.
- **Trigone muscle** — *Every day* · bladder & bowel. Keeps the ureter openings closed against backflow, especially while you urinate.
- **Internal urethral sphincter** — *Nonstop* · bladder & bowel, sexual function. Keeps the bladder neck closed around the clock; in males it also shuts during ejaculation.
- **Pubovesicalis** — *Every day* · bladder & bowel. Thought to help open the bladder neck when you urinate.
- **Rectovesicalis** — *Barely*. A band of smooth muscle between the bladder and rectum; no clear active role.

### Heart (cardiac)

- **Myocardium** — *Nonstop* · heartbeat. Contracts about 100,000 times a day without rest, and harder whenever you climb stairs or hurry.
- **Pectinate muscles** — *Nonstop* · heartbeat. Ridges of the atrial walls that contract with every beat.
- **Papillary muscles of right ventricle** — *Nonstop* · heartbeat. Tighten the cords of the tricuspid valve with every beat so the valve cannot flip back.
- **Papillary muscles of left ventricle** — *Nonstop* · heartbeat. Tighten the cords of the mitral valve with every beat.
- **Trabeculae carneae** — *Nonstop* · heartbeat. Muscle ridges of the ventricle walls that contract with every beat; the moderator band carries the heartbeat signal across the right ventricle.
