# Calisthenics Exercises

690 bodyweight exercises in 21 categories, from wall push-ups to the iron cross. Each has a difficulty level, the equipment it needs, and the main muscles it works, named to match muscles.json.

No list can hold every possible workout: the combinations never run out. The building blocks do, and this list catalogs them. It covers 690 distinct named exercises, the 16 variables that turn each one into many more, the 39 formats that arrange them into workouts, and 62 named workouts and 15 established programs built from them.

By level: 272 beginner, 211 intermediate, 150 advanced, 57 elite.

## How to read this list

- **Calisthenics** here means exercises where body weight provides the resistance, using at most simple apparatus: a bar, rings, parallel bars, parallettes, a box or bench, a wall or a pole. Added weight is a variable, not a separate entry.
- One entry per distinct named exercise. Generic tweaks (tempo, pauses, band assistance, added load) are listed once under *Variables* rather than repeated for every exercise.
- **Levels:** *beginner*: most healthy novices can do it now or within weeks. *intermediate*: needs a base of roughly 10 push-ups and a few pull-ups. *advanced*: months to years of focused training. *elite*: gymnastics-level strength or skill that few people ever reach. Levels are approximate.
- Tags after the level mark anything other than ordinary reps: *isometric* (held), *plyometric* (explosive), *conditioning*, *locomotion* (traveling), *mobility* and *skill*.
- Each group lists the main muscles (*Works*), secondary muscles (*Also*) and equipment. An entry repeats these only where it differs from its group.
- Groups marked *Easiest to hardest* are progressions: master each step before the next.
- In equipment, **/** separates alternatives and **+** joins items needed together.

## Push-ups

Horizontal pushing: pressing the body away from the floor, a wall or rings.

### Push-up progression

Works: Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii, Deltoid (anterior). Also: Serratus anterior, Abdominals. Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Wall push-up** *(beginner · wall)* — Hands on a wall, body leaning at a slight angle. Also called: Vertical push-up.
- **Incline push-up** *(beginner · box, bench or chair / low bar or sturdy table)* — Hands on a bench, box or bar; lower the surface over time to make it harder. Also called: Elevated push-up, Hands-elevated push-up.
- **Knee push-up** *(beginner)* — Push-up from the knees with the hips kept straight. Also called: Kneeling push-up, Modified push-up.
- **Negative push-up** *(beginner)* — Lower slowly from the top, then reset from the knees. Also called: Eccentric push-up.
- **Half push-up** *(beginner)* — Lower only until the elbows reach 90 degrees. Also called: Partial push-up.
- **Push-up** *(beginner)* — Body straight, chest to the floor, elbows about 45 degrees from the torso. Also called: Press-up, Standard push-up, Full push-up.
- **Decline push-up** *(intermediate · box, bench or chair)* — Feet on a box; shifts work toward the upper chest and shoulders. Also called: Feet-elevated push-up. Works: Pectoralis major (clavicular head), Deltoid (anterior), Triceps brachii.
- **Deficit push-up** *(intermediate · parallettes)* — Hands on parallettes or blocks so the chest sinks below the hands.

### Hand position & grip

Works: Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii, Deltoid (anterior). Also: Serratus anterior, Abdominals. Equipment: none.

- **Wide push-up** *(beginner)* — Hands well outside the shoulders; works the chest and triceps a little less than the standard push-up. Also called: Wide-grip push-up.
- **Close-grip push-up** *(beginner)* — Hands under the shoulders, elbows brushing the ribs: more triceps. Also called: Close push-up, Narrow push-up, Triceps push-up.
- **Diamond push-up** *(intermediate)* — Thumbs and index fingers touch to form a diamond under the chest. Also called: Triangle push-up. Works: Triceps brachii, Pectoralis major, Deltoid (anterior).
- **Staggered push-up** *(beginner)* — One hand forward and one back; switch sides each set. Also called: Offset push-up.
- **Knuckle push-up** *(beginner)* — On the fists with the wrists straight. Also called: Fist push-up.
- **Fingertip push-up** *(intermediate)* — On spread fingertips; strengthens the fingers and hands. Also: Serratus anterior, Abdominals, Forearm flexors, Intrinsic hand muscles.
- **Reverse-hand push-up** *(intermediate)* — Fingers point back toward the feet; loads the wrists and biceps as planche prep. Also called: Fingers-back push-up. Also: Biceps brachii, Forearm flexors.

### One-arm progression

Works: Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii, Deltoid (anterior). Also: Serratus anterior, Abdominals, Obliques. Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Archer push-up** *(intermediate)* — Lower toward one hand while the other arm stays straight out to the side.
- **Typewriter push-up** *(intermediate)* — At the bottom, slide from one hand to the other before pressing up.
- **Uneven push-up** *(intermediate)* — One hand on a raised block or ball, the other on the floor. Also called: Uneven-hands push-up.
- **Incline one-arm push-up** *(intermediate · box, bench or chair / low bar or sturdy table)* — One-arm push-up with the hand on a bench or bar; lower the surface to progress.
- **Lever push-up** *(advanced)* — One hand pushes while the other arm rests straight out on a ball or block.
- **Half one-arm push-up** *(advanced)* — One-arm push-up through the top half of the range.
- **One-arm push-up** *(advanced)* — Feet wide, working hand under the chest, free hand behind the back.
- **One-arm one-leg push-up** *(elite)* — One-arm push-up with the opposite foot lifted off the floor.

### Rings & leverage

Works: Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii, Deltoid (anterior). Also: Serratus anterior, Abdominals, Biceps brachii. Equipment: rings. Easiest to hardest.

- **Ring push-up** *(intermediate · rings / suspension trainer)* — Rings a few inches off the floor; the unstable handles work the stabilizers harder. Also called: Suspension push-up.
- **RTO ring push-up** *(advanced)* — Finish each rep with the rings turned out, palms facing forward. Also called: Rings-turned-out push-up.
- **Archer ring push-up** *(advanced)* — One ring slides out to the side while the other arm pushes. Also called: Ring archer push-up.
- **Bulgarian ring push-up** *(advanced)* — Let the rings travel wide at the bottom for a deep, fly-like stretch.
- **RTO pseudo planche push-up** *(advanced)* — Ring push-up with the rings turned out and the shoulders leaning well past the hands. Also called: RTO PPPU. Works: Deltoid (anterior), Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii. Also: Serratus anterior, Biceps brachii, Abdominals.
- **Lalanne push-up** *(elite · no equipment)* — Hands far out in front of the head; the body stays almost straight on a very long lever. Also called: Extended-arm push-up.

### Dynamic & compound push-ups

Works: Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii, Deltoid (anterior). Also: Serratus anterior, Abdominals. Equipment: none.

- **Hand-release push-up** *(beginner)* — Chest to the floor, lift the hands briefly, then press up. Also called: Release push-up, HRPU.
- **Shoulder-tap push-up** *(beginner)* — Tap the opposite shoulder at the top of each rep.
- **Single-leg push-up** *(beginner)* — One foot held off the floor for the whole set. Also called: One-leg push-up.
- **Push-up jack** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Jump the feet wide and back together with each rep.
- **Push-up to downward dog** *(beginner)* — After each push-up, push the hips up and back into a pike. Also called: Push-up to pike.
- **Walk-out push-up** *(beginner)* — Walk the hands out to a plank, do a push-up, walk back and stand. Also called: Inchworm push-up.
- **T push-up** *(beginner)* — Rotate into a side plank at the top, one arm to the ceiling. Also called: Rotational push-up, Push-up with rotation. Also: Serratus anterior, Abdominals, Obliques.
- **Spider-man push-up** *(intermediate)* — Bring one knee to the same-side elbow as you lower. Also called: Spiderman push-up. Also: Serratus anterior, Obliques, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **Walking push-up** *(intermediate)* — Walk the hands and feet sideways between reps. Also called: Lateral walking push-up.
- **Chaturanga** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Hold the bottom of a push-up a few inches off the floor, elbows hugging the ribs. Also called: Chaturanga dandasana, Low plank, Push-up bottom hold.
- **Hindu push-up** *(intermediate)* — From a pike, swoop the chest low along the floor up into a cobra, then push the hips straight back. Also called: Dand, Danda, Judo push-up. Also: Serratus anterior, Erector spinae, Abdominals.
- **Dive bomber push-up** *(intermediate)* — Swoop from a pike through a low arch into a cobra, then reverse the same path. Also: Serratus anterior, Erector spinae, Abdominals.

### Explosive push-ups

Works: Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii, Deltoid (anterior). Also: Serratus anterior, Abdominals. Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Incline plyo push-up** *(beginner · plyometric · box, bench or chair / wall)* — Push off a bench or wall hard enough for the hands to leave it. Also called: Incline pop-up.
- **Kneeling plyo push-up** *(beginner · plyometric)* — From the knees, push hard enough for the hands to leave the floor. Also called: Kneeling push-off.
- **Plyo push-up** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Push hard enough for the hands to leave the floor. Also called: Pop-up, Explosive push-up.
- **Clap push-up** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Clap once in the air before landing.
- **Lateral plyo push-up** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Explode sideways so the body travels across the floor. Also called: Traveling plyo push-up.
- **Depth push-up** *(advanced · plyometric · parallettes / box, bench or chair)* — Drop from two raised blocks to the floor and rebound straight back up. Also called: Drop push-up.
- **Chest-slap push-up** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Slap the chest in the air before landing. Also called: Chest-strike push-up.
- **Hip-slap push-up** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Slap the hips or thighs in the air before landing. Also called: Hip-strike push-up.
- **Double-clap push-up** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Two claps in the air before landing.
- **Behind-the-back clap push-up** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Clap behind the back in the air before landing. Also called: Back-clap push-up.
- **Full-body pop-up** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Hands and feet leave the floor at the same moment. Also called: Full-body plyo push-up.
- **Triple-clap push-up** *(elite · plyometric)* — Three claps in the air before landing.
- **Aztec push-up** *(elite · plyometric)* — Explode up, pike in the air to touch the toes, and land back in a plank.
- **Superman push-up** *(elite · plyometric)* — Launch the whole body off the floor with the arms flying forward overhead, then land in a push-up. Also called: Flying push-up.

### Chest flyes

Works: Pectoralis major, Deltoid (anterior). Also: Biceps brachii, Serratus anterior, Abdominals. Equipment: none.

- **Ring fly** *(intermediate · rings / suspension trainer)* — From a ring push-up position, let the arms open wide with soft elbows, then squeeze back. Also called: Ring flye, Suspension fly.
- **Slider fly** *(intermediate · sliders or towels)* — From a push-up or kneeling position, slide the hands apart and back together. Also called: Sliding chest fly.

## Overhead pressing & handstand push-ups

Vertical pushing: pressing the body up overhead, from pike push-ups to one-arm handstand push-ups.

### Pike & handstand push-up progression

Works: Deltoid (anterior), Deltoid (lateral), Triceps brachii, Trapezius (upper). Also: Serratus anterior, Pectoralis major (clavicular head), Abdominals. Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Dolphin push-up** *(beginner)* — From a forearm plank, push the hips up into a pike on the forearms, then return. Also called: Dolphin press. Works: Deltoid (anterior), Deltoid (lateral), Trapezius (upper). Also: Serratus anterior, Triceps brachii, Pectoralis major (clavicular head), Abdominals.
- **Pike push-up** *(intermediate)* — Hips high in an inverted V; lower the head to the floor just in front of the hands.
- **Elevated pike push-up** *(intermediate · box, bench or chair)* — Feet on a box so the torso is closer to vertical. Also called: Feet-elevated pike push-up, Decline pike push-up.
- **Deficit pike push-up** *(intermediate · parallettes)* — Hands on parallettes or blocks so the head can sink below the hands.
- **Box pike push-up** *(intermediate · box, bench or chair)* — Knees or feet on a box with the hips stacked over the shoulders. Also called: Box handstand push-up, Box push-up.
- **Handstand push-up negative** *(advanced · wall)* — Lower slowly from a wall handstand to the head, then come down. Also called: HSPU negative, Eccentric handstand push-up.
- **Half handstand push-up** *(advanced · wall)* — Wall handstand push-up through the top half of the range. Also called: Partial HSPU.
- **Kipping handstand push-up** *(advanced · wall)* — Drive the legs up from the bottom to help finish the press (CrossFit style). Also called: Kipping HSPU.
- **Wall handstand push-up** *(advanced · wall)* — Back to the wall, lower the head to the floor and press back to straight arms. Also called: HSPU, Handstand push-up, Handstand press-up, Wall headstand push-up, HeSPU.
- **Chest-to-wall handstand push-up** *(advanced · wall)* — Facing the wall, which forces a straighter body line. Also called: Wall-facing HSPU.
- **Close-grip handstand push-up** *(advanced · wall)* — Hands close together; more triceps, harder balance. Also called: Close handstand push-up.
- **Deficit handstand push-up** *(advanced · wall + parallettes)* — Hands on parallettes or plates so the head sinks below the hands. Also called: Deficit HSPU, Full-range HSPU.
- **Freestanding handstand push-up** *(elite)* — No wall: balance and press in the open. Also called: Freestanding HSPU, Freestanding HeSPU.
- **Ring handstand push-up** *(elite · rings)* — Handstand push-up on rings, often braced against the straps at first. Also called: Ring HSPU.
- **Uneven handstand push-up** *(elite · wall)* — One hand raised on a block or ball, shifting most of the load to the lower hand.
- **90-degree push-up** *(elite)* — From a handstand, lower into a bent-arm planche with the body horizontal, then press back up. Also called: 90° push-up. Also: Pectoralis major, Serratus anterior, Abdominals, Biceps brachii.
- **Tiger bend push-up** *(elite)* — From a handstand, lower onto the forearms and press back up to straight arms. Also called: Tiger bend.
- **One-arm handstand push-up** *(elite · wall)* — Wall-supported one-arm press; very few people have ever done a strict rep.

## Dips & support

Pressing down on bars or rings: support holds, dips and their harder variants.

### Support holds

Works: Triceps brachii, Deltoid (anterior), Pectoralis major, Trapezius (lower). Also: Serratus anterior, Abdominals. Equipment: parallel (dip) bars. Easiest to hardest.

- **Parallel bar support** *(beginner · isometric)* — Arms locked, shoulders pushed down away from the ears. Also called: Straight-arm support, Top dip hold.
- **Straight bar support** *(beginner · isometric · pull-up bar / low bar or sturdy table)* — Front support on a single bar at the hips, arms straight. Also called: Front support.
- **Bottom dip hold** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Hold the lowest point of a dip, shoulders just below the elbows.
- **Ring support hold** *(intermediate · isometric · rings)* — Rings pressed against the sides, arms locked, fighting the rings drifting away. Also called: Ring support.
- **RTO ring support** *(advanced · isometric · rings)* — Ring support with the rings turned out so the palms face forward. Also called: Rings-turned-out support.

### Dip progression

Works: Triceps brachii, Pectoralis major, Deltoid (anterior). Also: Serratus anterior, Trapezius (lower), Abdominals. Equipment: parallel (dip) bars. Easiest to hardest.

- **Floor dip** *(beginner · no equipment)* — Seated in a reverse tabletop with hands behind you; bend and straighten the arms. Also called: Crab dip.
- **Bench dip** *(beginner · box, bench or chair)* — Hands on a bench behind you, feet on the floor; keep the depth moderate. Also called: Chair dip.
- **Feet-elevated bench dip** *(beginner · box, bench or chair)* — Bench dip with the feet up on a second bench.
- **Negative dip** *(beginner)* — Lower slowly from the top of the bars. Also called: Eccentric dip.
- **Parallel bar dip** *(intermediate)* — Lower until the shoulders pass below the elbows; lean forward for chest, stay upright for triceps. Also called: Dip, Chest dip, Triceps dip.
- **Straight bar dip** *(intermediate · pull-up bar / low bar or sturdy table)* — Dip on a single bar in front of the hips; a key muscle-up strength. Also called: Single-bar dip.
- **Ring dip** *(advanced · rings)* — Dip on rings, keeping them close to the body.
- **L-sit dip** *(advanced)* — Dip while holding the legs straight out in an L. Also: Serratus anterior, Abdominals, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **Korean dip** *(advanced · pull-up bar / low bar or sturdy table)* — Dip on a straight bar with the bar behind the body.
- **Russian dip** *(advanced)* — At the bottom of a dip, roll back onto the forearms, then return and press out.
- **RTO ring dip** *(advanced · rings)* — Ring dip finishing each rep with the rings turned out.
- **Bulgarian ring dip** *(advanced · rings)* — Let the rings travel wide at the bottom for a deep, fly-like range.
- **Impossible dip** *(elite)* — From the forearms resting along the bars, press up to straight arms without swinging: the dip-bar cousin of the tiger bend.

### Triceps isolation

Works: Triceps brachii. Also: Anconeus, Abdominals. Equipment: none.

- **Sphinx push-up** *(intermediate)* — From a forearm plank, press up onto the hands by straightening only the elbows. Also called: Triceps extension push-up.
- **Bodyweight triceps extension** *(intermediate · low bar or sturdy table / rings / suspension trainer / box, bench or chair)* — Hands on a bar, bench or rings; bend the elbows to bring the head under the hands, then extend. Also called: Bodyweight skull crusher, Bar triceps extension, Ring triceps extension.

## Pull-ups & chin-ups

Vertical pulling: hanging from a bar or rings and pulling the body up.

### Hangs & scapular pulls

Works: Latissimus dorsi, Forearm flexors, Trapezius (lower). Also: Teres major, Rhomboids, Abdominals. Equipment: pull-up bar. Easiest to hardest.

- **Dead hang** *(beginner · isometric)* — Hang with straight arms and relaxed shoulders. Also called: Passive hang, Bar hang.
- **Active hang** *(beginner · isometric)* — Straight arms with the shoulders pulled down away from the ears. Also called: Scapular hang.
- **Scapular pull-up** *(beginner)* — From a dead hang, pull the shoulder blades down and back without bending the arms. Also called: Scap pull-up, Hanging scapular shrug, Straight-arm pull.
- **Flexed-arm hang** *(beginner · isometric)* — Hold the chin above the bar. Also called: Top hold, Chin-over-bar hold. Works: Latissimus dorsi, Biceps brachii, Brachialis, Forearm flexors.
- **Arch hang** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Hang with the chest pushed toward the bar and the shoulder blades squeezed together. Also called: Arch body hang. Works: Trapezius (lower), Trapezius (middle), Rhomboids, Latissimus dorsi.

### Pull-up progression

Works: Latissimus dorsi, Biceps brachii, Brachialis, Teres major. Also: Rhomboids, Trapezius (lower), Deltoid (posterior), Forearm flexors, Abdominals. Equipment: pull-up bar. Easiest to hardest.

- **Jumping pull-up** *(beginner)* — Jump to the top position, then lower under control.
- **Negative pull-up** *(beginner)* — Start with the chin over the bar and lower as slowly as possible. Also called: Eccentric pull-up.
- **Assisted pull-up** *(beginner)* — A band, a box under the feet or a partner takes part of the weight. Also called: Band-assisted pull-up, Foot-assisted pull-up.
- **Half pull-up** *(beginner)* — Pull-ups through the top half of the range only. Also called: Partial pull-up.
- **Chin-up** *(intermediate)* — Palms facing you, hands about shoulder width; more biceps. Also called: Chin, Supinated pull-up.
- **Neutral-grip pull-up** *(intermediate)* — Palms facing each other on parallel handles. Also called: Hammer-grip pull-up, Parallel-grip pull-up.
- **Pull-up** *(intermediate)* — Palms facing away, hands a little wider than the shoulders; chin clears the bar. Also called: Pronated pull-up, Overhand pull-up.
- **Wide-grip pull-up** *(intermediate)* — Hands well outside the shoulders; shorter range, more lats.
- **Close-grip pull-up** *(intermediate)* — Hands close together. Also called: Close pull-up.
- **Mixed-grip pull-up** *(intermediate)* — One hand overhand, one underhand; switch sides each set. Also called: Alternating-grip pull-up.
- **Commando pull-up** *(intermediate)* — Stand under the bar lengthwise with the hands close and staggered; pull the head to alternate sides.
- **Ring pull-up** *(intermediate · rings)* — On rings, which let the hands rotate freely.
- **Chest-to-bar pull-up** *(intermediate)* — Pull until the chest touches the bar. Also called: C2B pull-up.
- **Behind-the-neck pull-up** *(advanced)* — Pull the bar behind the head; needs good shoulder mobility.
- **L-sit pull-up** *(advanced)* — Pull-up while holding the legs straight out in an L. Also: Rhomboids, Trapezius (lower), Forearm flexors, Rectus abdominis, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **Towel pull-up** *(advanced · pull-up bar + towel)* — Pull-up holding towels draped over the bar; very hard on the grip. Also: Rhomboids, Trapezius (lower), Deltoid (posterior), Forearm flexors, Intrinsic hand muscles, Abdominals.
- **Sternum pull-up** *(advanced)* — Lean back and pull until the lower chest touches the bar. Also called: Gironda pull-up, Sternum chin-up, Arch-body pull-up. Also: Rhomboids, Trapezius (middle), Deltoid (posterior), Forearm flexors, Abdominals.
- **Archer pull-up** *(advanced)* — Pull toward one hand while the other arm straightens along the bar.
- **Typewriter pull-up** *(advanced)* — At the top, slide from one hand to the other with the chin over the bar.
- **Around-the-world pull-up** *(advanced)* — Trace a circle over the bar: up on one side, across the top, down the other.
- **Uneven pull-up** *(advanced · pull-up bar + towel)* — One hand on the bar, the other lower on a towel or strap.
- **Assisted one-arm pull-up** *(advanced)* — One hand on the bar; the other helps as little as possible from a towel, the wrist or fingertips. Also called: Assisted one-arm chin-up.
- **One-arm negative** *(elite)* — Lower from the top on one arm as slowly as possible. Also called: One-arm pull-up negative.
- **One-arm pull-up** *(elite)* — Pull-up on one arm; usually done with an underhand or neutral grip. Also called: One-arm chin-up, OAP, OAC.

### Kipping & explosive pulls

Works: Latissimus dorsi, Biceps brachii, Brachialis, Teres major. Also: Rhomboids, Trapezius (lower), Abdominals. Equipment: pull-up bar.

- **Kipping pull-up** *(intermediate)* — A hip swing helps drive the body up (CrossFit style).
- **Butterfly pull-up** *(advanced)* — A continuous cycling kip that circles the body under the bar (CrossFit style).
- **Explosive pull-up** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Pull as fast as possible, aiming the chest past the bar. Also called: Power pull-up.
- **High pull-up** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Pull explosively until the bar reaches the waist; the bridge to a muscle-up. Also called: Waist pull-up, Hip pull-up.
- **Clap pull-up** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Let go at the top, clap, and catch the bar.
- **Switch-grip pull-up** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Let go at the top and switch from overhand to underhand before catching. Also called: Grip-switch pull-up.
- **Headbanger pull-up** *(advanced)* — At the top, push the body back from the bar and pull it in again without dropping. Also called: Headbangers.

### Climbing & traverses

Works: Latissimus dorsi, Biceps brachii, Brachialis, Forearm flexors. Also: Teres major, Abdominals. Equipment: climbing rope. Easiest to hardest.

- **Monkey-bar traverse** *(beginner · locomotion · monkey bars)* — Swing hand over hand along a row of rungs. Also called: Brachiation, Monkey bars.
- **Lateral bar traverse** *(intermediate · locomotion · pull-up bar)* — Shuffle the hands sideways along a single bar without letting go with both. Also called: Bar traverse.
- **Heel hook** *(intermediate · pull-up bar)* — From an alternating-grip hang, pull with the arms, lift the feet over the bar and cross the ankles around it; lower slowly (Army drill). Works: Rectus abdominis, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Latissimus dorsi, Forearm flexors.
- **Rope climb** *(intermediate)* — Climb the rope using a foot lock or leg wrap. Also called: Rope climb with legs.
- **Legless rope climb** *(advanced)* — Climb the rope with the arms alone.

## Rows

Horizontal pulling: pulling the chest toward a bar, rings or a fixed handhold.

### Row progression

Works: Latissimus dorsi, Rhomboids, Trapezius (middle), Deltoid (posterior), Biceps brachii, Brachialis. Also: Teres major, Forearm flexors, Abdominals. Equipment: low bar or sturdy table / rings / suspension trainer. Easiest to hardest.

- **Door-frame row** *(beginner · door frame or sturdy post)* — Hold both sides of a door frame, lean back with straight arms and pull yourself in. Also called: Vertical pull, Doorway row, Vertical row.
- **Table row** *(beginner · low bar or sturdy table)* — Lie under a sturdy table, grip the edge and pull the chest up to it. Also called: Tabletop row.
- **Incline row** *(beginner)* — Rings or a high bar with the body at a steep angle; walk the feet forward to make it harder. Also called: Incline ring row, High row.
- **Bent-knee inverted row** *(beginner)* — Inverted row with the knees bent and the feet flat on the floor.
- **Inverted row** *(beginner)* — Body straight under a hip-height bar or rings, heels down; pull the chest to the hands. Also called: Australian pull-up, Bodyweight row, Body row, Ring row, Suspension row, TRX row.
- **Underhand inverted row** *(beginner)* — Palms facing you; more biceps. Also called: Reverse-grip row, Supinated row.
- **Wide-grip inverted row** *(intermediate)* — Hands wide; more upper back and rear delts. Also called: Wide row. Works: Rhomboids, Trapezius (middle), Deltoid (posterior), Latissimus dorsi. Also: Brachialis, Teres major, Forearm flexors, Abdominals.
- **Feet-elevated inverted row** *(intermediate · low bar or sturdy table + box, bench or chair / rings + box, bench or chair)* — Feet on a box so the body is horizontal or tilted head-down. Also called: Elevated row.
- **Archer row** *(advanced)* — Row toward one hand while the other arm stays straight. Also called: Archer inverted row.
- **One-arm inverted row** *(advanced)* — Row on one hand, keeping the hips and shoulders square. Also called: One-arm ring row, Single-arm row.

### Upper-back & rear-delt isolation

Works: Deltoid (posterior), Trapezius (middle), Rhomboids. Also: Infraspinatus & teres minor, Trapezius (lower), Abdominals. Equipment: rings / suspension trainer.

- **Ring face pull** *(beginner)* — Lean back from the rings and pull them toward the forehead, elbows high and wide. Also called: Face pull, Suspension face pull.
- **Ring reverse fly** *(intermediate)* — Lean back holding the rings with straight arms and open them into a T. Also called: Ring rear-delt fly, Suspension reverse fly, Ring T-raise.
- **Ring Y-raise** *(intermediate)* — Lean back and raise the straight arms overhead into a Y. Also called: Suspension Y-raise. Works: Trapezius (lower), Deltoid (posterior), Deltoid (lateral).
- **Ring W-raise** *(intermediate)* — Pull the rings back and apart with bent elbows, squeezing the shoulder blades into a W. Also called: Suspension W-raise. Works: Trapezius (middle), Trapezius (lower), Rhomboids, Infraspinatus & teres minor.
- **Scapular row** *(beginner)* — In an inverted-row position, pinch the shoulder blades together and apart without bending the arms. Works: Rhomboids, Trapezius (middle). Also: Trapezius (lower), Deltoid (posterior).

## Arms, scapula & shoulder health

Isolation and prehab: bodyweight curls, shoulder-blade control and rotator-cuff work.

### Biceps

Works: Biceps brachii, Brachialis, Brachioradialis. Also: Forearm flexors, Abdominals. Equipment: rings / suspension trainer / low bar or sturdy table. Easiest to hardest.

- **Bodyweight biceps curl** *(intermediate)* — Lean back holding rings or a low bar, palms up, and curl the body in toward the hands. Also called: Ring curl, Suspension curl, Bar curl.
- **Pelican curl** *(elite · rings)* — Rings behind the body: lean forward on straight arms, then curl back up with the biceps.

### Scapular control & rotator cuff

Works: Trapezius (lower), Trapezius (middle), Rhomboids. Also: Serratus anterior, Infraspinatus & teres minor. Equipment: none.

- **Scapular push-up** *(beginner)* — In a plank, let the chest sink between the shoulder blades, then push the floor away. Also called: Push-up plus, Scap push-up. Works: Serratus anterior, Pectoralis minor. Also: Abdominals.
- **Scapular dip** *(beginner · parallel (dip) bars)* — In a dip support, let the shoulders rise toward the ears, then press them down. Also called: Dip shrug, Support shrug, Reverse shrug. Works: Trapezius (lower), Pectoralis minor, Latissimus dorsi.
- **Scapular wall slide** *(beginner · wall)* — Arms against a wall, slide them up and down without losing contact. Also called: Wall slide, Wall angel. Works: Trapezius (lower), Serratus anterior, Trapezius (middle).
- **Prone I raise** *(beginner)* — Face down, raise the straight arms overhead in line with the body. Also called: I-raise. Works: Trapezius (lower), Deltoid (posterior).
- **Prone Y raise** *(beginner)* — Face down, raise the straight arms overhead in a Y, thumbs up. Also called: Y-raise. Works: Trapezius (lower), Deltoid (posterior). Also: Infraspinatus & teres minor, Erector spinae.
- **Prone T raise** *(beginner)* — Face down, raise the straight arms out to the sides in a T. Also called: T-raise. Works: Trapezius (middle), Rhomboids, Deltoid (posterior).
- **Prone W raise** *(beginner)* — Face down with bent elbows, squeeze the shoulder blades to lift the arms into a W. Also called: W-raise. Works: Trapezius (middle), Trapezius (lower), Rhomboids, Infraspinatus & teres minor.
- **Prone L raise** *(beginner)* — Face down with elbows bent 90 degrees, rotate the forearms up to lift the hands. Also called: L-raise, Prone external rotation. Works: Infraspinatus & teres minor, Trapezius (middle).
- **Prone row** *(beginner)* — Face down, pull the elbows back as if rowing and squeeze the shoulder blades. Also called: Floor row. Works: Rhomboids, Trapezius (middle), Deltoid (posterior).
- **Reverse snow angel** *(beginner)* — Face down, sweep the straight arms from the hips to overhead and back, hovering them off the floor. Works: Trapezius (lower), Trapezius (middle), Deltoid (posterior), Rhomboids. Also: Infraspinatus & teres minor, Erector spinae.
- **Handstand shrug** *(intermediate · wall)* — In a wall handstand, push tall through the shoulders, then sink, without bending the arms. Also called: Wall handstand shrug. Works: Trapezius (upper), Serratus anterior. Also: Deltoid (anterior), Triceps brachii, Abdominals.

## Muscle-ups & bar transitions

Getting from below the bar or rings to above it, plus the swings, kips and circles of bar work.

### Muscle-up progression

Works: Latissimus dorsi, Biceps brachii, Triceps brachii, Pectoralis major, Deltoid (anterior). Also: Teres major, Trapezius (lower), Forearm flexors, Abdominals. Equipment: pull-up bar. Easiest to hardest.

- **Low-bar muscle-up** *(intermediate · low bar or sturdy table)* — On a chest-height bar with the feet helping, practice pulling up and turning over the bar. Also called: Low-bar transition, Seated muscle-up transition.
- **Ring transition drill** *(intermediate · rings)* — Rings low and feet on the floor: lean back in a false grip and roll the chest over the hands into a dip. Also called: Kneeling muscle-up transition.
- **False-grip hang** *(intermediate · isometric · rings)* — Hang with the wrists wrapped over the rings so the heels of the hands carry the weight. Works: Forearm flexors, Latissimus dorsi, Biceps brachii.
- **False-grip row** *(intermediate · rings)* — Inverted ring row keeping the false grip throughout. Works: Latissimus dorsi, Biceps brachii, Forearm flexors, Rhomboids.
- **Jumping muscle-up** *(intermediate)* — Jump to help carry the body over the bar, then lower slowly. Also called: Assisted muscle-up.
- **Negative muscle-up** *(advanced)* — From the support on top, lower slowly through the transition to a hang. Also called: Eccentric muscle-up.
- **Kipping muscle-up** *(advanced)* — A swing drives the hips up and the chest over the bar. Also called: Kipping bar muscle-up, Bar muscle-up (CrossFit).
- **Muscle-up** *(advanced)* — Strict pull from a dead hang, through the transition, to a dip-out on top of the bar. Also called: Bar muscle-up, Strict muscle-up.
- **Kipping ring muscle-up** *(advanced · rings)* — Swing-assisted muscle-up on rings.
- **Ring muscle-up** *(advanced · rings)* — Strict muscle-up on rings using a false grip. Also called: Strict ring muscle-up.
- **Wide-grip muscle-up** *(advanced)* — Hands well outside the shoulders, making the transition longer and harder.
- **L-sit muscle-up** *(elite)* — Muscle-up with the legs held straight out in an L throughout. Also: Teres major, Trapezius (lower), Forearm flexors, Rectus abdominis, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **Slow muscle-up** *(elite)* — A deliberately slow, strict muscle-up with no swing anywhere. Also called: Slow-motion muscle-up.
- **Reverse-grip muscle-up** *(elite)* — Muscle-up with the palms facing you. Also called: Chin-up-grip muscle-up, Supinated muscle-up.
- **Clap muscle-up** *(elite · plyometric)* — Explode high enough over the bar to clap before catching the support.
- **Hefesto** *(elite)* — From a German hang with the arms straight behind you, curl up until the bar sits behind your back in a Korean-dip support: a backward muscle-up. Also called: German hang muscle-up. Works: Biceps brachii, Brachialis, Latissimus dorsi, Deltoid (anterior). Also: Triceps brachii, Forearm flexors, Abdominals.
- **One-arm muscle-up** *(elite)* — Muscle-up on one arm; only a small number of athletes have done one.

### Swings, kips & hip circles

Works: Latissimus dorsi, Abdominals, Deltoid (anterior). Also: Triceps brachii, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Forearm flexors, Gluteus maximus. Equipment: pull-up bar. Easiest to hardest.

- **Tap swing** *(intermediate)* — A controlled hollow-to-arch swing that builds the rhythm for kips and releases. Also called: Kip swing, Beat swing, Hollow-arch swing. Works: Latissimus dorsi, Abdominals, Forearm flexors.
- **Pull-over** *(intermediate)* — From a hang, pull the hips up and over the bar to finish in a front support. Also called: Bar pull-over. Works: Latissimus dorsi, Biceps brachii, Abdominals, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **Back hip circle** *(intermediate)* — From a front support, circle backward around the bar with the hips against it. Works: Abdominals, Latissimus dorsi, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **Cast** *(intermediate)* — From a front support, swing the legs back and push the hips away so the body rises off the bar. Works: Latissimus dorsi, Deltoid (anterior), Triceps brachii, Abdominals.
- **Front hip circle** *(advanced)* — From a front support, fall forward around the bar and circle back up to support. Also called: Forward hip circle. Works: Abdominals, Latissimus dorsi, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **Glide kip** *(advanced)* — Glide forward under the bar, bring the ankles to it, then pull through to a front support. Also called: Kip.
- **Back uprise** *(advanced · rings / parallel (dip) bars)* — Use the backswing to rise from a hang or upper-arm hang up to a support.
- **Front uprise** *(advanced · rings / parallel (dip) bars)* — Use the forward swing to rise from a hang up to a support.
- **Inlocate** *(advanced · rings)* — On rings, rotate backward around the shoulders from a hang into an inverted pike. Works: Latissimus dorsi, Abdominals, Deltoid (posterior).
- **Dislocate** *(advanced · rings)* — On rings, shoot the legs up and out from an inverted pike and rotate forward to a hang, arms turning out. Works: Latissimus dorsi, Abdominals, Deltoid (posterior).
- **Clear hip circle** *(elite · high bar (gymnastics))* — A back hip circle with the hips clear of the bar, finishing near a handstand.
- **Giant swing** *(elite · high bar (gymnastics))* — A full circle around the bar in a straight, handstand-like line. Also called: Giant.

### Freestyle releases & spins

Works: Latissimus dorsi, Biceps brachii, Forearm flexors, Abdominals. Also: Triceps brachii, Deltoid (anterior), Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris). Equipment: pull-up bar. Easiest to hardest.

- **Swing 180** *(advanced · plyometric)* — From a swing, let go and turn 180 degrees to regrab the bar facing the other way.
- **360 pull-up** *(elite · plyometric)* — Explosive pull-up, let go, spin a full turn in the air and regrab the bar.
- **Swing 360** *(elite · plyometric)* — From a swing, let go, spin a full turn and regrab.

## Levers, planche & flags

Static straight-arm strength: holding the body horizontal (or sideways) off the floor, a bar, rings or a pole.

### Planche progression

Works: Deltoid (anterior), Pectoralis major, Serratus anterior. Also: Biceps brachii, Triceps brachii, Abdominals, Gluteus maximus, Forearm flexors. Equipment: parallettes optional. Easiest to hardest.

- **Planche lean** *(intermediate · isometric)* — From a straight-arm push-up position, lean the shoulders well forward of the hands. Also called: Pseudo planche lean.
- **Tuck planche** *(advanced · isometric)* — Knees tucked to the chest, hips at shoulder height, feet off the floor.
- **Advanced tuck planche** *(advanced · isometric)* — Tuck planche with a flat back and the hips pushed out behind. Also called: Adv. tuck planche, Flat-back tuck planche.
- **One-leg planche** *(advanced · isometric)* — One leg straight behind, the other tucked.
- **90-degree hold** *(advanced · isometric)* — Hold a bent-arm planche: elbows at 90 degrees, body straight and horizontal. Also called: Bent-arm planche, 90° hold. Also: Biceps brachii, Triceps brachii, Abdominals, Gluteus maximus.
- **Straddle planche** *(elite · isometric)* — Legs straight and spread wide.
- **Half-lay planche** *(elite · isometric)* — Hips extended with the knees bent, a shorter lever than the full planche.
- **Full planche** *(elite · isometric)* — Body straight and horizontal, balanced on straight arms. Also called: Planche, Full-lay planche.
- **Floor maltese** *(elite · isometric)* — Planche with the hands wide and turned out near the hips, the body just above the floor. Also called: Maltese planche.
- **One-arm planche** *(elite · isometric)* — A planche balanced on one arm; only a handful of people have held one.

### Planche push-ups & presses

Works: Deltoid (anterior), Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii, Serratus anterior. Also: Biceps brachii, Abdominals. Equipment: parallettes optional. Easiest to hardest.

- **Pseudo planche push-up** *(intermediate)* — Push-up with the hands by the hips and the shoulders leaning well past them. Also called: PPPU, Planche lean push-up.
- **Tuck planche push-up** *(advanced)* — Push-ups while holding a tuck planche.
- **Advanced tuck planche push-up** *(elite)* — Push-ups while holding an advanced tuck planche.
- **Straddle planche push-up** *(elite)* — Push-ups while holding a straddle planche.
- **Full planche push-up** *(elite)* — Push-ups while holding a full planche. Also called: Planche push-up.
- **Maltese push-up** *(elite)* — Planche push-up with the hands wide by the hips and the body skimming the floor.
- **Planche press to handstand** *(elite)* — From a planche, press up to a handstand without bending the arms. Also called: Straight-arm planche press.

### Front lever progression

Works: Latissimus dorsi, Teres major, Deltoid (posterior), Trapezius (lower), Rectus abdominis. Also: Rhomboids, Triceps brachii, Biceps brachii, Forearm flexors, Abdominals. Equipment: pull-up bar / rings. Easiest to hardest.

- **Tuck front lever** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Under the bar on straight arms, knees tucked to the chest, back horizontal.
- **Advanced tuck front lever** *(advanced · isometric)* — Tuck with a flat back and the hips opened to 90 degrees. Also called: Adv. tuck front lever.
- **One-leg front lever** *(advanced · isometric)* — One leg straight, the other tucked.
- **Straddle front lever** *(advanced · isometric)* — Legs straight and spread wide.
- **Half-lay front lever** *(advanced · isometric)* — Hips straight, knees bent.
- **Front lever** *(advanced · isometric)* — Body straight and horizontal under the bar, face up, arms straight. Also called: Full front lever.
- **One-arm front lever** *(elite · isometric)* — A front lever held on one arm.

### Front lever dynamics

Works: Latissimus dorsi, Teres major, Deltoid (posterior), Trapezius (lower), Rectus abdominis. Also: Biceps brachii, Rhomboids, Abdominals. Equipment: pull-up bar / rings. Easiest to hardest.

- **Tuck front lever row** *(intermediate)* — Hold a tuck front lever and row the chest to the bar.
- **Front lever raise** *(advanced)* — From a hang, raise the straight body to front-lever height with straight arms. Also called: Front lever lift.
- **Ice cream maker** *(advanced)* — From the top of a pull-up, extend the arms and lower into a front lever, then pull back up.
- **Advanced tuck front lever row** *(advanced)* — Rows while holding an advanced tuck front lever.
- **Straddle front lever row** *(elite)* — Rows while holding a straddle front lever.
- **Front lever row** *(elite)* — Row the chest to the bar while holding a full front lever.
- **Front lever pull-up** *(elite)* — Pull the bar down toward the hips while the body stays horizontal in a front lever.

### Back lever progression

Works: Latissimus dorsi, Pectoralis major, Deltoid (anterior), Biceps brachii. Also: Deltoid (posterior), Abdominals, Erector spinae, Gluteus maximus, Trapezius (lower). Equipment: rings / pull-up bar. Easiest to hardest.

- **Inverted hang** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Hang upside down with the body and arms straight. Works: Latissimus dorsi, Abdominals.
- **German hang** *(intermediate · isometric)* — From an inverted hang, lower the hips behind until the arms stretch back behind the body. Also called: Skin-the-cat hold. Works: Pectoralis major, Deltoid (anterior), Biceps brachii.
- **Skin the cat** *(intermediate)* — From a hang, tuck and rotate backward through an inverted hang into a German hang, then return.
- **Tuck back lever** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Face down under the rings, knees tucked, back horizontal, arms straight.
- **Advanced tuck back lever** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Tuck with a flat back and the hips opened.
- **Straddle back lever** *(advanced · isometric)* — Legs straight and spread wide.
- **Half-lay back lever** *(advanced · isometric)* — Hips straight, knees bent.
- **Back lever** *(advanced · isometric)* — Body straight and horizontal, face down, arms straight behind the body. Also called: Full back lever.
- **Back lever pull-out** *(advanced)* — From a German hang, pull through the back lever up to an inverted hang. Also called: Back lever pull.
- **One-arm back lever** *(elite · isometric)* — A back lever held on one arm.

### Human flag progression

Works: Obliques, Latissimus dorsi, Deltoid (lateral), Quadratus lumborum. Also: Abdominals, Gluteus medius & minimus, Triceps brachii, Serratus anterior. Equipment: vertical pole or stall bars. Easiest to hardest.

- **Clutch flag** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Hug the pole with the top arm and shoulder while the bottom arm presses; hold the body out sideways. Works: Obliques, Quadratus lumborum, Latissimus dorsi. Also: Abdominals, Gluteus medius & minimus.
- **Vertical flag** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Top arm pulls, bottom arm pushes, body held straight up beside the pole. Also called: Flag support.
- **Tuck human flag** *(advanced · isometric)* — Knees tucked with the torso horizontal beside the pole.
- **Straddle human flag** *(advanced · isometric)* — Legs straight and spread.
- **Human flag** *(advanced · isometric)* — Body straight and horizontal, held out from the pole by the arms alone. Also called: Full human flag, Press flag, Flagpole.
- **Human flag raise** *(elite)* — Raise the straight body from vertical to horizontal and back. Also called: Flag raise.

### Rings strength elements

Works: Pectoralis major, Latissimus dorsi, Biceps brachii. Also: Teres major, Deltoid (anterior), Triceps brachii, Serratus anterior, Abdominals. Equipment: rings. Easiest to hardest.

- **Ring planche** *(elite · isometric)* — A full planche on rings, rings turned out. Also called: Planche on rings. Works: Deltoid (anterior), Pectoralis major, Serratus anterior. Also: Biceps brachii, Triceps brachii, Abdominals.
- **Iron cross** *(elite · isometric)* — Hang with the arms straight out to the sides, hands level with the shoulders. Also called: Cross.
- **Inverted cross** *(elite · isometric)* — An iron cross held upside down. Works: Pectoralis major, Latissimus dorsi, Deltoid (anterior).
- **Azarian** *(elite)* — A slow, straight-arm backward roll that finishes in an iron cross.
- **Nakayama** *(elite)* — Rise from a back lever up into an iron cross with straight arms.
- **Maltese** *(elite · isometric)* — Face-down horizontal hold with straight arms spread wide and the rings near the hips. Also called: Maltese cross, Hirondelle, Swallow. Works: Deltoid (anterior), Pectoralis major, Latissimus dorsi, Serratus anterior.
- **Victorian** *(elite · isometric)* — Face-up horizontal hold at ring height, straight arms angled out from the sides with the rings near the hips: an upside-down maltese. Also called: Victorian cross, Inverted swallow. Works: Latissimus dorsi, Teres major, Pectoralis major. Also: Deltoid (posterior), Triceps brachii, Biceps brachii, Abdominals, Gluteus maximus.

## Handstands, inversions & arm balances

Balancing on the hands, head or forearms.

### Handstand progression

Works: Deltoid (anterior), Deltoid (lateral), Triceps brachii, Trapezius (upper), Serratus anterior. Also: Forearm flexors, Abdominals, Trapezius (lower). Equipment: wall. Easiest to hardest.

- **Box pike hold** *(beginner · isometric · box, bench or chair)* — Feet on a box with the hips stacked over the shoulders and the arms straight. Also called: Pike handstand hold.
- **Wall walk** *(intermediate)* — From a push-up position with the feet at the wall, walk the feet up and the hands in, then back down. Also called: Wall climb.
- **Chest-to-wall handstand** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Walk up the wall facing it and hold a straight line. Also called: Wall-facing handstand.
- **Back-to-wall handstand** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Kick up with the back to the wall and hold. Also called: Wall handstand.
- **Handstand kick-up** *(intermediate · wall optional)* — Kick up from a lunge into a handstand, alternating the lead leg.
- **Wall handstand shoulder tap** *(intermediate)* — Chest to the wall, shift onto one hand and tap the opposite shoulder. Also called: Handstand shoulder taps.
- **Heel pull** *(intermediate · isometric)* — In a back-to-wall handstand, pull the heels off the wall to find balance.
- **Toe pull** *(intermediate · isometric)* — In a chest-to-wall handstand, pull the toes off the wall to find balance.
- **Freestanding handstand** *(advanced · isometric · no equipment)* — A straight handstand in the open, balanced through the fingers and wrists. Also called: Handstand.
- **Tuck handstand** *(advanced · isometric · no equipment)* — A handstand with the knees tucked to the chest.
- **Straddle handstand** *(advanced · isometric · no equipment)* — A handstand with the legs spread wide.
- **Handstand walk** *(advanced · locomotion · no equipment)* — Walk on the hands forward, backward or sideways. Also called: Hand walk.
- **Parallette handstand** *(advanced · isometric · parallettes)* — A handstand on parallettes, balancing through the grip.
- **Handstand pirouette** *(advanced · no equipment)* — Walk the hands around in a turn while staying in the handstand.
- **Ring handstand** *(elite · isometric · rings)* — A handstand on rings, usually learned with the feet braced on the straps first.
- **Hollowback handstand** *(elite · isometric · no equipment)* — A handstand with the legs lowered behind and the back deeply arched. Also called: Hollowback.
- **One-arm handstand** *(elite · isometric · no equipment)* — A handstand balanced on one hand. Also called: OAHS.

### Press to handstand

Works: Deltoid (anterior), Serratus anterior, Triceps brachii, Abdominals. Also: Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Trapezius (lower), Trapezius (upper), Forearm flexors. Equipment: parallettes optional. Easiest to hardest.

### Headstands, forearm stands & shoulder stands

Works: Deltoid (anterior), Trapezius (upper), Abdominals. Also: Neck extensors (splenius, semispinalis), Triceps brachii, Serratus anterior. Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Shoulder stand** *(beginner · isometric)* — On the upper back with the hips and legs straight up, hands supporting the back. Also called: Candlestick, Sarvangasana. Works: Abdominals, Gluteus maximus, Erector spinae. Also: Neck extensors (splenius, semispinalis).

- **Ring shoulder stand** *(intermediate · isometric · rings)* — Support on the rings with the shoulders down beside them and the body straight up. Works: Deltoid (anterior), Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii, Abdominals.
- **Scorpion pose** *(advanced · isometric)* — A forearm stand with the back arched and the feet lowered toward the head. Also called: Vrischikasana. Also: Neck extensors (splenius, semispinalis), Triceps brachii, Serratus anterior, Erector spinae.

### Arm balances

Works: Deltoid (anterior), Triceps brachii, Pectoralis major, Abdominals. Also: Serratus anterior, Forearm flexors, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris). Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Frog stand** *(beginner · isometric)* — Squat with the hands down and the knees resting on the elbows; lean forward until the feet lift. Also called: Frog balance.
- **Crow pose** *(beginner · isometric)* — Knees on the backs of the upper arms; lean forward and lift the feet. Also called: Crow, Kakasana.

- **Pendant pose** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Kneel with the ankles crossed, then press the hands down to lift the knees and feet. Also called: Lolasana. Works: Abdominals, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Deltoid (anterior), Triceps brachii.
- **Eight-angle pose** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Legs hooked around one arm and crossed at the ankles, body leaning forward on bent arms. Also called: Astavakrasana. Also: Serratus anterior, Obliques, Adductors.
- **Firefly pose** *(intermediate · isometric)* — From a wide squat, sit the thighs on the arms and extend the legs forward off the floor. Also called: Tittibhasana. Also: Serratus anterior, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Adductors.
- **Shoulder-pressing pose** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Legs wrapped over the upper arms with the ankles crossed in front, feet off the floor. Also called: Bhujapidasana. Also: Serratus anterior, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Adductors.
- **Elephant's trunk pose** *(intermediate · isometric)* — One leg hooked over the upper arm and the other straight forward, both lifted with the hips. Also called: Eka hasta bhujasana. Also: Serratus anterior, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **Scale pose** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Seated in lotus, press the hands down beside the hips and lift the whole body. Also called: Tolasana. Also: Serratus anterior, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **Elbow lever** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Elbows pressed into the hips, body horizontal and balanced on the hands. Also: Serratus anterior, Forearm flexors, Erector spinae, Gluteus maximus.
- **Flying pigeon** *(advanced · isometric)* — One shin across the upper arms, the other leg extended straight back. Also called: Eka pada galavasana.
- **Flying splits** *(advanced · isometric)* — Balanced on bent arms with one leg extended forward over an arm and the other straight back. Also called: Eka pada koundinyasana II.
- **Rooster pose** *(advanced · isometric)* — In lotus, thread the arms through the gaps between the thighs and calves and lift. Also called: Kukkutasana. Also: Serratus anterior, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **Peacock pose** *(advanced · isometric)* — Hands together with the fingers turned back, elbows in the belly, body horizontal. Also called: Mayurasana. Also: Serratus anterior, Forearm flexors, Erector spinae, Gluteus maximus.
- **One-arm elbow lever** *(advanced · isometric)* — An elbow lever balanced on one arm. Also: Obliques, Forearm flexors, Erector spinae.

## Core

Trunk strength: resisting and creating movement through the spine and pelvis.

## Legs: squats, lunges & single-leg

Knee-dominant lower-body work, from assisted squats to pistols and shrimp squats.

### Squat progression

Works: Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus, Adductors. Also: Hamstrings, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus), Erector spinae, Abdominals. Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Shoulderstand squat** *(beginner)* — Lying on the upper back with the legs overhead, bend the knees toward the forehead and straighten them. Also: Abdominals.
- **Jackknife squat** *(beginner · box, bench or chair)* — Hands on a knee-high support in front; bend at the hips and knees to squat, helping with the arms.
- **Assisted squat** *(beginner · door frame or sturdy post / vertical pole or stall bars / rings / suspension trainer)* — Hold a door frame, pole or rings and squat deep, using the arms as needed. Also called: Supported squat, Suspension squat.
- **Box squat** *(beginner · box, bench or chair)* — Sit back to touch a box or chair, then stand. Also called: Sit-to-stand, Chair squat.
- **Half squat** *(beginner)* — Squat until the knees bend to about 90 degrees. Also called: Partial squat, Knee bender.
- **Bodyweight squat** *(beginner)* — Feet about shoulder width; sit the hips down between the heels, thighs below parallel if you can. Also called: Air squat, Squat, Full squat.
- **Prisoner squat** *(beginner)* — Hands behind the head, elbows pulled back. Also: Hamstrings, Erector spinae, Trapezius (middle), Abdominals.
- **Sumo squat** *(beginner)* — Wide stance with the toes turned out. Also called: Wide squat, Plié squat, Deep sumo squat.
- **Close squat** *(beginner)* — Feet together or nearly so. Also called: Narrow squat.
- **Hindu squat** *(beginner)* — Heels rise at the bottom as the arms swing back, then drive up as they swing forward; done for high reps. Also called: Baithak, Bethak. Also: Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus), Hamstrings, Abdominals.
- **Uneven squat** *(intermediate)* — One foot raised on a block or ball so the lower leg does most of the work. Also called: Staggered squat.

### Single-leg squats

Works: Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus. Also: Adductors, Hamstrings, Gluteus medius & minimus, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus), Abdominals. Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Assisted pistol squat** *(intermediate · door frame or sturdy post / vertical pole or stall bars / rings / suspension trainer)* — Hold a door frame, pole or rings and use the arms as needed. Also called: Supported pistol, Assisted one-leg squat.
- **Box pistol squat** *(intermediate · box, bench or chair)* — Single-leg squat down to a box or bench and back up. Also called: Single-leg box squat, Single-leg sit-to-stand.
- **Beginner shrimp squat** *(intermediate)* — Lower until the rear knee touches the floor, the rear toes touching too; arms forward for balance.
- **Skater squat** *(intermediate)* — Lower the free knee toward the floor behind you with the torso leaning forward. Also called: Airborne lunge.
- **Elevated pistol squat** *(intermediate · box, bench or chair)* — Stand on a box so the free leg can drop below it at the bottom.
- **Pistol squat** *(intermediate)* — Squat all the way down on one leg with the other held straight out in front. Also called: Pistol, One-leg squat, Single-leg squat.
- **Intermediate shrimp squat** *(intermediate)* — Touch down with the rear knee alone, the rear toes kept off the floor. Also called: Shrimp squat.
- **Advanced shrimp squat** *(advanced)* — Hold the rear foot with the same-side hand and touch down with the knee.
- **Two-hand shrimp squat** *(advanced)* — Hold the rear foot with both hands behind the back and touch down with the knee.
- **Deficit shrimp squat** *(advanced · box, bench or chair)* — Shrimp squat standing on a step so the knee travels below the foot. Also called: Elevated shrimp squat.
- **Dragon pistol squat** *(advanced)* — A pistol with the free leg passed behind and around the standing leg. Also called: Dragon squat.
- **Hawaiian squat** *(advanced)* — Single-leg squat with the free ankle resting on the standing thigh in a figure-4. Also called: Figure-4 squat.

### Split squats & lunges

Works: Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus. Also: Adductors, Hamstrings, Gluteus medius & minimus, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus), Abdominals. Equipment: none.

- **Split squat** *(beginner)* — Staggered stance; drop the back knee straight down toward the floor. Also called: Static lunge.

- **Bulgarian split squat** *(intermediate · box, bench or chair)* — Rear foot on a bench behind you; drop the back knee toward the floor. Also called: Rear-foot-elevated split squat, RFESS.
- **Front-foot-elevated split squat** *(intermediate · box, bench or chair)* — Front foot on a low step for a deeper range.

### Step-ups

- **Donkey kick** *(beginner)* — On hands and knees, drive one bent leg up and back. Also called: Quadruped hip extension, Glute kickback. Works: Gluteus maximus, Hamstrings. Also: Multifidus, Abdominals.
- **Back extension** *(beginner · GHD or back-extension bench)* — On a 45-degree or horizontal bench, lower the torso and raise it back in line with the legs. Also called: Hyperextension, 45-degree back extension.
- **Arch rock** *(intermediate)* — Rock back and forth while holding the arch shape. Also called: Superman rock.
- **Hip airplane** *(intermediate)* — Hinged forward on one leg, rotate the pelvis open to the side and back closed. Works: Gluteus medius & minimus, Gluteus maximus, Deep hip rotators. Also: Hamstrings, Abdominals.
- **Reverse hyperextension** *(intermediate · box, bench or chair / GHD or back-extension bench)* — Torso on a bench or table with the hips at the edge; raise the straight legs to horizontal. Also called: Reverse hyper. Works: Gluteus maximus, Hamstrings, Erector spinae. Also: Multifidus, Abdominals.

### Hip abductors, adductors & flexors

Works: Gluteus medius & minimus, Tensor fasciae latae. Also: Gluteus maximus, Deep hip rotators, Abdominals. Equipment: none.

- **Clamshell** *(beginner)* — Side-lying with the knees bent, open the top knee without rolling the hips back. Works: Gluteus medius & minimus, Deep hip rotators.
- **Fire hydrant** *(beginner)* — On hands and knees, lift one bent leg out to the side.
- **Side-lying leg raise** *(beginner)* — Lying on one side, lift the straight top leg. Also called: Side-lying hip abduction, Lateral leg raise.
- **Side-lying adductor raise** *(beginner)* — Lying on one side, lift the bottom leg. Also called: Inner-thigh raise, Medial leg raise. Works: Adductors. Also: Abdominals.
- **Hip hike** *(beginner · box, bench or chair)* — Stand on one leg on a step; let the free hip drop, then lift it using the standing hip. Also called: Pelvic drop.
- **Seated knee lift** *(beginner · box, bench or chair)* — Sitting tall on a bench, lift one knee above hip level and hold briefly. Also called: Seated psoas lift. Works: Iliopsoas. Also: Rectus femoris, Abdominals.
- **Short-lever Copenhagen plank** *(intermediate · isometric · box, bench or chair)* — Side plank with the top knee on a bench and the bottom leg hanging free. Works: Adductors. Also: Obliques, Gluteus medius & minimus, Abdominals.
- **Copenhagen plank** *(advanced · isometric · box, bench or chair)* — Side plank with the top foot on a bench and the bottom leg free. Also called: Copenhagen adductor plank. Works: Adductors. Also: Obliques, Gluteus medius & minimus, Abdominals.
- **Copenhagen adduction** *(advanced · box, bench or chair)* — From the Copenhagen plank, lower the body and lift it back with the top leg. Also called: Copenhagen hip adduction. Works: Adductors. Also: Obliques, Gluteus medius & minimus, Abdominals.

## Calves, shins & feet

Lower-leg and foot strength for the ankles, arches and toes.

### Calf raises

Works: Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus). Also: Fibularis longus & brevis, Tibialis posterior, Intrinsic foot muscles. Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Double-leg calf raise** *(beginner)* — Rise onto the balls of both feet, then lower slowly. Also called: Calf raise, Standing calf raise.
- **Bent-knee calf raise** *(beginner)* — Knees bent throughout, which shifts the work to the soleus. Also called: Soleus raise. Works: Soleus. Also: Gastrocnemius, Fibularis longus & brevis.
- **Deficit calf raise** *(beginner · box, bench or chair)* — Balls of the feet on a step, heels dropping below it at the bottom. Also called: Step calf raise, Elevated calf raise.
- **Eccentric heel drop** *(beginner · box, bench or chair)* — Rise on both feet, then lower slowly on one with the heel sinking below the step. Also called: Alfredson heel drop.
- **Single-leg calf raise** *(intermediate · wall optional)* — A calf raise on one leg, a hand on the wall for balance.
- **Single-leg deficit calf raise** *(intermediate · box, bench or chair)* — A single-leg calf raise from a step with a full stretch at the bottom.

### Shins, ankles & feet

Works: Tibialis anterior. Also: Long toe extensors, Fibularis longus & brevis, Intrinsic foot muscles. Equipment: none.

- **Tibialis raise** *(beginner · wall)* — Back against a wall with the heels forward of the hips; lift the toes and forefoot as high as possible. Also called: Wall tib raise, Toe raise, Reverse calf raise. Works: Tibialis anterior, Long toe extensors.
- **Heel walk** *(beginner · locomotion)* — Walk on the heels with the toes lifted. Works: Tibialis anterior, Long toe extensors.
- **Toe walk** *(beginner · locomotion)* — Walk on the balls of the feet with the heels high. Also called: Walk on toes, Tiptoe walk. Works: Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus), Fibularis longus & brevis, Intrinsic foot muscles.
- **Backward walk** *(beginner · locomotion)* — Walk backward, pushing off the balls of the feet. Also called: Retro walking. Works: Quadriceps, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus). Also: Tibialis anterior, Gluteus maximus.
- **Short foot** *(beginner)* — Draw the ball of the foot toward the heel to lift the arch without curling the toes. Also called: Foot doming, Short-foot exercise. Works: Intrinsic foot muscles. Also: Tibialis posterior.
- **Toe yoga** *(beginner)* — Lift the big toe while the others stay down, then swap. Works: Intrinsic foot muscles, Long toe extensors, Long toe flexors.
- **Toe spread** *(beginner · isometric)* — Spread the toes as wide as possible and hold. Also called: Toe spread-out. Works: Abductor hallucis, Abductor digiti minimi (foot), Dorsal interossei (foot).
- **Towel scrunch** *(beginner · towel)* — Pull a towel toward you along the floor using only the toes. Also called: Towel curl, Toe curl. Works: Intrinsic foot muscles, Long toe flexors.
- **Single-leg balance** *(beginner · isometric)* — Stand on one leg; progress with the eyes closed or on a soft surface. Also called: Stork stand. Works: Intrinsic foot muscles, Fibularis longus & brevis, Tibialis posterior, Gluteus medius & minimus.

## Neck

Strength for the neck flexors, extensors and side benders, from isometrics to bridges.

### Neck isometrics & raises

Works: Neck flexors (SCM, longus colli & capitis), Neck extensors (splenius, semispinalis). Also: Scalenes, Trapezius (upper), Levator scapulae. Equipment: none.

- **Chin tuck** *(beginner)* — Glide the head straight back into a double chin, then release. Also called: Cervical retraction, Lying neck retraction. Works: Deep neck flexors (longus colli & capitis).
- **Hand-resisted neck isometric** *(beginner · isometric)* — Press the head into your hand forward, back and to each side without letting it move. Also called: Self-resisted neck isometric, Manual neck isometric.
- **Lying neck curl** *(beginner)* — On the back (head off a bench edge, if you use one), tuck the chin and lift the head. Also called: Supine neck flexion. Works: Neck flexors (SCM, longus colli & capitis).
- **Prone neck extension** *(beginner)* — Face down (head off a bench edge, if you use one), lift the head back. Also called: Lying neck extension. Works: Neck extensors (splenius, semispinalis), Trapezius (upper).
- **Side-lying neck raise** *(beginner)* — Lying on one side, lift the head toward the ceiling. Also called: Lateral neck raise. Works: Sternocleidomastoid, Scalenes. Also: Levator scapulae, Trapezius (upper).
- **Wall neck bridge** *(beginner · isometric · wall)* — Lean the head against a padded wall, front, back and each side in turn, holding part of your weight. Also called: Wall neck isometric.

### Neck bridges

Works: Neck extensors (splenius, semispinalis), Neck flexors (SCM, longus colli & capitis). Also: Trapezius (upper), Erector spinae, Gluteus maximus, Abdominals. Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Front neck bridge** *(intermediate · isometric)* — Face down on the forehead and toes, hands nearby for support; build up gradually. Also called: Forehead bridge, Front bridge. Works: Neck flexors (SCM, longus colli & capitis). Also: Scalenes, Abdominals.
- **Wrestler's bridge** *(advanced · isometric)* — Arched back, weight on the back of the head and the feet; use the hands at first, and skip it with a neck problem. Also called: Back neck bridge, Rear neck bridge. Works: Neck extensors (splenius, semispinalis). Also: Trapezius (upper), Erector spinae, Gluteus maximus, Hamstrings.
- **Neck bridge rock** *(advanced)* — In a front or back neck bridge, rock forward and back over the head. Also called: Bridge rocks.

## Grip, wrists & forearms

Hand, finger and wrist strength from hangs, fingertip work and wrist loading.

### Hangs & grip

Works: Forearm flexors, Intrinsic hand muscles. Also: Latissimus dorsi, Brachioradialis. Equipment: pull-up bar. Easiest to hardest.

- **One-arm towel hang** *(advanced · isometric · pull-up bar + towel)* — Hang from a single towel with one hand.
- **Ledge shimmy** *(advanced · locomotion · ledge or hangboard)* — Traverse sideways along a ledge hanging from the fingertips. Also called: Ledge traverse.

### Fingers & wrists

Works: Forearm flexors, Forearm extensors, Intrinsic hand muscles. Equipment: none.

- **Towel wring** *(beginner · towel)* — Twist a rolled towel hard in both directions, as if wringing it out. Works: Forearm flexors, Forearm extensors. Also: Pronator teres, Supinator, Brachioradialis.
- **Back-of-hand push-up** *(advanced)* — Push-up on the backs of the hands, fingers pointing toward each other; build up from the knees. Also called: Wrist push-up, Reverse wrist push-up. Works: Forearm extensors. Also: Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii.
- **Two-finger push-up** *(elite)* — Push-up on the thumb and index finger of each hand; the one-arm version is a famous feat. Works: Forearm flexors, Intrinsic hand muscles. Also: Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii, Deltoid (anterior).

## Plyometrics & jumps

Explosive lower-body work: jumping, hopping, bounding and landing.

### Jumps in place

Works: Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus). Also: Hamstrings, Abdominals. Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Pogo hop** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Quick, stiff-legged hops off the balls of the feet. Also called: Pogo jump, Ankle hop, Two-foot ankle hop, Stiff-knee jump, Straight hop. Works: Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus). Also: Quadriceps, Tibialis anterior.
- **Squat jump** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Squat, then jump as high as possible and land softly back into the squat. Also called: Jump squat, Power jump.
- **Countermovement jump** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Dip quickly and jump straight up, reaching overhead. Also called: Vertical jump, Jump and reach, Vertical leap.
- **High jumper** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Swing the arms back and forth with small hops, taking a higher jump every few reps (Army drill).
- **Star jump** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Jump up spreading the arms and legs into an X, then land with the feet together. Also called: Starburst jump, Spread-eagle jump.
- **Rotational jump** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Jump and turn 90, 180 or 360 degrees, landing in balance. Also called: Jump turn, 180 jump, 360 jump, Alternate quarter-turn jump.
- **Split jump** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — From a lunge, jump and switch legs in the air. Also called: Jumping lunge, Switch lunge, Split squat jump, Scissor jump, Alternate staggered squat jump.
- **Tuck jump** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Jump and pull the knees to the chest. Also called: Knee-tuck jump. Also: Hamstrings, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Rectus abdominis.
- **Pike jump** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Jump and lift the straight legs forward to reach for the toes. Also: Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Rectus abdominis.
- **Butt-kick jump** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Jump and kick the heels up to the glutes. Also: Hamstrings, Abdominals.
- **Heel-click jump** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Jump and click the heels together in the air.
- **Kneeling jump** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — From kneeling, swing the arms and jump up onto the feet. Also called: Kneeling squat jump.
- **Single-leg hop** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Repeated hops on one leg in place. Also called: Single-leg pogo, Hop in place.
- **Single-leg tuck jump** *(advanced · plyometric)* — A tuck jump taking off and landing on one leg.
- **Jumping pistol squat** *(advanced · plyometric)* — A pistol squat that explodes into a one-leg jump. Also called: Pistol jump.

### Horizontal & lateral jumps

Works: Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus). Also: Hamstrings, Adductors, Gluteus medius & minimus, Abdominals. Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Frog jump** *(beginner · plyometric)* — From a deep squat with the hands down, jump forward and land back in the squat. Also called: Frog hop, Frog jumps forward and backward.
- **Bunny hop** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Continuous small two-footed hops forward. Also called: Two-foot hops.
- **Lateral hop** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Hop side to side over a line with both feet. Also called: Line hop, Ski jump, Lateral line hop.
- **Broad jump** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Swing the arms and jump forward as far as possible, landing on both feet. Also called: Standing long jump.
- **Skater jump** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Bound sideways from one foot to the other, the trailing leg swinging behind. Also called: Skater hop, Speed skater, Lateral bound.
- **Quadrant jump** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Jump around a square pattern (forward, sideways, back, sideways) on both feet or one. Also called: Dot drill, Box-pattern hop.
- **Hurdle hop** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Jump continuously over a row of low obstacles such as cones. Also called: Barrier hop, Hurdle jump.
- **Standing triple jump** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — From a standing start: hop, step, then jump into a two-foot landing.
- **Bounding** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Exaggerated running strides, driving the knee up and hanging in the air as long as possible. Also called: Alternate-leg bound, Double-arm alternate-leg bound.
- **Single-leg bound** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Repeated long hops on the same leg for distance. Also called: Single-leg hop for distance.

### Skips

Works: Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus), Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris). Also: Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus, Hamstrings, Abdominals. Equipment: none.

- **A-skip** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Skip with a high knee drive and an active, pawing foot strike.
- **Power skip** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Skip for maximum height, driving the knee and the opposite arm up. Also called: Power skipping.
- **Lateral skip** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Skip sideways, pushing off the trailing foot. Also called: Side skip.
- **Backward skip** *(beginner · plyometric)* — Skip moving backward.
- **B-skip** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — An A-skip that extends the lead leg forward before pawing it back down. Also: Hamstrings, Gluteus maximus, Abdominals.

### Box & depth jumps

Works: Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus). Also: Hamstrings, Abdominals. Equipment: box, bench or chair. Easiest to hardest.

- **Box push-off** *(beginner · plyometric)* — One foot on a box, push off it to jump as high as possible and land with the same or other foot up. Also called: Single-leg push-off, Alternate-leg push-off.
- **Lateral box push-off** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Box beside you: push off the top foot, jump across the box and land with the other foot on top. Also called: Side-to-side push-off.
- **Box jump** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Jump from the floor onto a box, landing softly; step down.
- **Seated box jump** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — From seated on a bench, jump straight onto a box.
- **Lateral box jump** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Jump sideways onto a box.
- **Box jump-over** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Jump onto and over a box, or clear it completely.
- **Depth drop** *(intermediate · plyometric)* — Step off a box and stick the landing. Also called: Drop landing, Snap-down.
- **Depth jump** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Step off a box and rebound into a maximal jump the instant you land. Also called: Drop jump, Shock jump.
- **Depth jump to box** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Step off one box and rebound straight onto a second.
- **Single-leg box jump** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Jump onto a box taking off and landing on one leg.
- **Single-leg depth jump** *(advanced · plyometric)* — Step off a low box onto one leg and rebound into a jump.

## Conditioning & cardio

Continuous whole-body work that drives the heart rate up: jacks, running drills, climbers and burpees.

### Jacks & in-place cardio

Works: Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus), Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus. Also: Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Deltoid (lateral), Abdominals. Equipment: none.

- **Jumping jack** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Jump the feet wide as the arms swing overhead, then back together. Also called: Side-straddle hop, Half jack (arms only to shoulder height).
- **Step jack** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Step one foot out at a time as the arms rise: a jack without the jump. Also called: Low-impact jack.
- **Seal jack** *(beginner · conditioning)* — A jumping jack with the arms opening wide and clapping together in front at chest height. Also: Pectoralis major, Deltoid (posterior), Abdominals.
- **Cross jack** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Jump the feet out, then cross them as they land, alternating which foot is in front. Also called: Crisscross jack, Criss-cross.
- **Split jack** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Jump between split stances, switching the front foot, while the opposite arm swings forward. Also called: Scissor jack.
- **Squat jack** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Jump the feet wide into a squat, then back together.
- **Plank jack** *(beginner · conditioning)* — In a high plank, jump the feet out and in. Works: Abdominals, Deltoid (anterior). Also: Gluteus maximus, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus), Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **High knees** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Run in place or forward, driving the knees to hip height. Also called: High-knee run, Verticals. Works: Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus), Quadriceps.
- **Butt kicks** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Run in place, kicking the heels up to the glutes. Also called: Heel flicks. Works: Hamstrings, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus).
- **Running in place** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Jog on the spot, lifting the feet clear of the floor. Also called: Stationary run, Jogging in place.
- **Fast feet** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Tiny, rapid steps on the balls of the feet in a low athletic stance. Also called: Quick feet, Chop feet, Football chops.
- **Shadow boxing** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Throw punch combinations in the air while moving the feet. Works: Deltoid (anterior), Abdominals, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus). Also: Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii, Obliques.
- **Lateral shuffle** *(beginner · locomotion)* — Stay low and shuffle sideways without crossing the feet. Also called: Side shuffle, Laterals.
- **Carioca** *(beginner · locomotion)* — Move sideways, crossing the trailing foot alternately in front of and behind the lead foot. Also called: Grapevine, Crossovers. Also: Obliques, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Abdominals.
- **Shuttle run** *(beginner · locomotion)* — Sprint back and forth between two lines, touching each line at the turn. Also called: Suicides, Line drill, Shuttle sprint. Works: Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus, Hamstrings, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus).

### Mountain climbers

Works: Abdominals, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris). Also: Deltoid (anterior), Quadriceps, Serratus anterior. Equipment: none.

- **Mountain climber** *(beginner · conditioning)* — In a high plank, drive the knees toward the chest in a running rhythm.
- **Incline mountain climber** *(beginner · conditioning · box, bench or chair)* — Hands on a bench for an easier angle.
- **Cross-body mountain climber** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Drive each knee toward the opposite elbow. Also called: Twisting mountain climber, Cross-over climber. Also: Obliques, Deltoid (anterior), Quadriceps.
- **Slider mountain climber** *(beginner · conditioning · sliders or towels)* — Slide the feet alternately on sliders instead of lifting them.

### Burpees & sprawls

Works: Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus, Pectoralis major. Also: Triceps brachii, Deltoid (anterior), Abdominals, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus). Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Squat thrust** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Squat, hands down, jump the feet back to a plank, jump them in, stand. Also called: Four-count burpee, Half burpee, Original burpee (1939).
- **Burpee** *(beginner · conditioning)* — A squat thrust with a push-up at the bottom and a jump at the top. Also called: Burpee with push-up.
- **Sprawl** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Drop the hips to the floor as the legs shoot back, then snap back up to standing.
- **Up-down** *(beginner · conditioning)* — Run in place, drop flat to the chest, then pop straight back up and keep running. Also called: Football up-down.
- **Eight-count bodybuilder** *(intermediate · conditioning)* — A squat thrust with a push-up, plus opening and closing the feet in the plank. Also called: 8-count bodybuilder, Eight-count push-up.
- **Lateral burpee** *(intermediate · conditioning)* — Jump sideways over a line or low object after each burpee. Also called: Lateral burpee over.
- **Burpee broad jump** *(intermediate · conditioning)* — Broad jump forward out of every burpee.
- **Burpee tuck jump** *(intermediate · conditioning)* — Finish each burpee with a tuck jump.
- **Burpee box jump** *(intermediate · conditioning · box, bench or chair)* — Jump onto a box at the top of each burpee.
- **Burpee pull-up** *(intermediate · conditioning · pull-up bar)* — Jump to a pull-up at the top of each burpee. Also: Latissimus dorsi, Biceps brachii, Triceps brachii, Deltoid (anterior), Abdominals.
- **Single-leg burpee** *(advanced · conditioning)* — A burpee done entirely on one leg.

## Locomotion & animal movement

Traveling on hands and feet: crawls, animal walks and Animal Flow.

### Crawls & animal walks

Works: Abdominals, Deltoid (anterior), Quadriceps. Also: Serratus anterior, Triceps brachii, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Gluteus maximus. Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Baby crawl** *(beginner · locomotion)* — Crawl on hands and knees, moving opposite hand and knee together. Also called: Hands-and-knees crawl.
- **Bear crawl** *(beginner · locomotion)* — On hands and feet with the knees just off the floor, move opposite limbs together in any direction. Also called: Bear walk, Traveling beast.
- **Crab walk** *(beginner · locomotion)* — Face up on hands and feet with the hips lifted, walk in any direction. Also called: Traveling crab. Works: Triceps brachii, Deltoid (posterior), Gluteus maximus. Also: Hamstrings, Abdominals.
- **Inchworm** *(beginner · locomotion)* — From standing, walk the hands out to a plank, then walk the feet up to the hands. Also called: Walkout, Inch worm. Works: Abdominals, Deltoid (anterior), Hamstrings.
- **Duck walk** *(beginner · locomotion)* — Walk while staying in a deep squat. Also called: Duck waddle. Works: Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus. Also: Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus), Adductors, Abdominals.
- **Monkey walk** *(beginner · locomotion)* — From a deep squat, place both hands to one side and hop the feet over past them. Also called: Lateral monkey, Lateral traveling ape. Also: Adductors, Gluteus maximus, Triceps brachii.
- **Frogger** *(beginner · locomotion)* — Hands on the floor in front; hop the feet up outside the hands into a deep squat, chest up, then reach forward again. Also: Adductors, Gluteus maximus, Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus).
- **Wheelbarrow walk** *(beginner · locomotion · partner)* — Walk on the hands while a partner holds your legs. Works: Deltoid (anterior), Triceps brachii, Abdominals.
- **Army crawl** *(beginner · locomotion)* — Face down, pull with the forearms and push with the legs, belly on the ground. Also called: Low crawl, Commando crawl. Works: Latissimus dorsi, Deltoid (anterior), Abdominals. Also: Gluteus maximus, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **High crawl** *(beginner · locomotion)* — On the forearms and knees with the body off the ground, moving opposite elbow and knee together.
- **Lame dog walk** *(intermediate · locomotion)* — Crawl on both hands and one foot, the other leg held up behind. Also called: Three-legged crawl, Lame dog.
- **Leopard crawl** *(intermediate · locomotion)* — A bear crawl with the hips and knees very low, just off the floor. Also called: Leopard.
- **Lizard crawl** *(intermediate · locomotion)* — Crawl at the bottom of a push-up, bringing each knee up toward its elbow. Also called: Spiderman crawl, Crocodile crawl. Also: Pectoralis major, Triceps brachii, Obliques, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris).
- **Seal walk** *(intermediate · locomotion)* — In a high plank with the legs relaxed, walk forward on the hands, dragging the feet. Also called: Seal crawl. Works: Deltoid (anterior), Triceps brachii, Abdominals.

### Animal Flow

Works: Abdominals, Deltoid (anterior), Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus. Also: Serratus anterior, Triceps brachii, Obliques, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris). Equipment: none.

- **Loaded beast** *(beginner)* — From the beast (hands and feet, knees hovering), rock the hips back toward the heels and forward again.
- **Crab reach** *(beginner · mobility)* — From a static crab, drive the hips up and reach one arm overhead and behind into a backbend. Works: Gluteus maximus, Erector spinae, Obliques. Also: Triceps brachii, Deltoid (posterior).
- **Scorpion reach** *(intermediate)* — From loaded beast, shift forward, then circle one bent leg out and up behind you, shin to the sky, spine twisting like a scorpion tail.
- **Underswitch** *(intermediate)* — From the beast, thread one leg under the body and turn over into a crab, then switch back.
- **Side kickthrough** *(intermediate)* — From the beast, rotate and kick one leg through under the body while the opposite hand lifts.

## Tumbling & acrobatics

Floor gymnastics and acrobatic skills done with body weight alone.

### Rolls, cartwheels & flips

Works: Abdominals, Quadriceps, Gluteus maximus. Also: Deltoid (anterior), Triceps brachii, Hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), Calves (gastrocnemius, soleus). Equipment: none. Easiest to hardest.

- **Forward roll** *(beginner · skill)* — Tuck the chin, place the hands and roll over the backs of the shoulders to stand. Also called: Forward somersault.

- **Cartwheel** *(beginner · skill)* — Hand, hand, foot, foot: travel sideways through a straddled handstand.
- **Dive roll** *(intermediate · skill)* — Jump forward into a forward roll, the hands catching first. Also called: Long dive.
- **Handstand forward roll** *(intermediate · skill)* — From a handstand, bend the arms, tuck the chin and roll out.

## Variables that multiply every exercise

Apply any of these, alone or combined, to almost any exercise above. This is why the number of possible workouts has no end.

- **Leverage** — Move the load further from the pivot to make a move harder, or closer to make it easier. *e.g.* incline push-up, push-up, decline push-up; tuck, straddle, full front lever
- **Range of motion** — Work through a partial range, the full range, or a deficit range with the hands or feet raised. *e.g.* half push-up, deficit push-up, ATG split squat
- **Tempo** — Set a time for each phase (lowering, bottom pause, lifting, top pause), written like 3-1-1-0; X means explosive. *e.g.* 5-second-lowering pull-ups
- **Pauses & isometric holds** — Stop and hold at any point in the range, most often the hardest point. *e.g.* pause squat, 90-degree pull-up hold, bottom-of-dip hold
- **Eccentric only** — Do only the lowering phase, as slowly as possible; builds strength for moves not yet possible. *e.g.* negative pull-up, Nordic curl negative, negative muscle-up
- **1½ reps & partials** — Add a half rep inside each full rep, or finish a set with partial reps in the stretched range. *e.g.* 1½ squats, bottom-half pistol pulses
- **Assistance** — Let a band, the feet, a partner, a box or a jump take part of the load. *e.g.* band-assisted pull-up, foot-assisted pistol, jumping muscle-up
- **Added load** — Wear a vest, dip belt, backpack or ankle weights. Strictly speaking this is no longer pure bodyweight. *e.g.* weighted pull-up, vested Murph
- **Unilateral & asymmetric loading** — Shift the work toward one limb: one-arm or one-leg, archer, uneven, staggered or typewriter. *e.g.* archer push-up, uneven pull-up, pistol squat
- **Grip & hand position** — Change width, orientation (overhand, underhand, neutral, mixed), false grip, fingertips, knuckles, fists or towels. *e.g.* wide-grip pull-up, false-grip ring row, knuckle push-up
- **Stance & foot position** — Change stance width and toe angle, split or stagger the feet, or raise the front or rear foot. *e.g.* sumo squat, Bulgarian split squat
- **Surface & stability** — Use rings or straps, one leg, a narrow base or closed eyes so the same move is harder to stabilize. *e.g.* ring push-up, single-leg glute bridge
- **Explosive intent** — Move as fast as possible, or add a flight phase with a clap, jump or release. *e.g.* clap push-up, squat jump, clap pull-up
- **Kipping or strict** — Use a swing or hip drive for speed and volume, or move strictly with no momentum. *e.g.* kipping pull-up versus strict pull-up
- **Plane & direction** — Move forward, backward, sideways or with rotation. *e.g.* lateral lunge, reverse bear crawl, T push-up
- **Combinations** — Chain two or more movements into a single rep. *e.g.* burpee pull-up, push-up to shoulder tap, lunge to knee drive

## Workout formats

Ways to arrange exercises into sets, rounds and time.

- **Straight sets** — Fixed sets and reps of one exercise with fixed rest, finished before moving on. *e.g.* 3 × 8, 90 s rest
- **Circuit** — One set of each of several exercises in a row with little rest, repeated for rounds. *e.g.* the Minimalist Routine; the 7-Minute Workout
- **Superset** — Two exercises back to back with no rest between, either opposing (push/pull) or the same muscles. *e.g.* dips then rows
- **Tri-set & giant set** — Three, or four or more, exercises back to back before resting.
- **Couplet & triplet** — A workout built from two or three repeated movements. *e.g.* Wilmot: squats and ring dips
- **EMOM** — Start a set at the top of every minute; the rest of the minute is rest. *e.g.* Chelsea
- **E2MOM & alternating EMOM** — The same on a 2- or 3-minute clock, or switching movements on odd and even minutes.
- **AMRAP** — As many rounds or reps as possible in a fixed time; scored in rounds plus reps. *e.g.* Cindy
- **For time** — A fixed amount of work done as fast as possible, usually with a time cap. *e.g.* Angie
- **Chipper** — A long list of high-rep movements done once through, in order. *e.g.* Angie, Bert
- **Buy-in & cash-out** — A fixed task done before or after the main work. *e.g.* Maxim 56's 56-second handstand hold
- **Partition** — A large rep total split up however you like, or in a fixed pattern. *e.g.* Murph's middle section as 20 rounds of 5/10/15
- **Accumulation** — Reach a total of reps or hold time in as many sets as it takes. *e.g.* 100 pull-ups in as few sets as possible
- **Tabata** — 20 s of work and 10 s of rest, 8 times (4 minutes); CrossFit scores the lowest round. *e.g.* Tabata Something Else
- **HIIT intervals** — Repeated hard bouts at a fixed work-to-rest ratio. *e.g.* 30:30, 40:20 or 30:60
- **Timed sets** — Maximum reps of one exercise in a set time. *e.g.* 1 minute of push-ups
- **Interval sets** — At the start of each 1- to 3-minute interval, work close to failure, then rest out the interval. *e.g.* Mark Lauren's You Are Your Own Gym
- **Station rotation** — A fixed time at each station, rotating on the clock. *e.g.* Manuel
- **Ascending ladder** — Reps climb each set (1, 2, 3 and up) to a target or to just short of failure.
- **Descending ladder & countdown** — Reps drop each set (10, 9, down to 1). *e.g.* 21-15-9; 50-40-30-20-10
- **Up-and-down ladder & pyramid** — Reps climb, then come back down. *e.g.* 1-2-3-4-5-4-3-2-1
- **Density training** — A fixed block (often 15 minutes) alternating two exercises for as many total reps as possible; beat it next time. *e.g.* Charles Staley's Escalating Density Training
- **Rest-pause** — Take a set close to failure, rest 10–20 s, and do more reps; repeat once to three times.
- **Myo-reps** — One set near failure, then mini-sets of 3–5 reps separated by 3–5 deep breaths.
- **Cluster sets** — A set broken into small groups of 1–5 reps with short rests so every rep stays sharp.
- **Mechanical drop set** — At or near failure, switch with no rest to an easier version of the same movement. *e.g.* archer push-ups, then push-ups, then incline push-ups
- **Wave loading** — Repeat a short sequence of sets that gets harder each set, then start again.
- **Grease the Groove** — Frequent sets of about half your max spread through the day, never near failure. *e.g.* Pavel Tsatsouline's method for pull-ups or pistols
- **Every hour on the hour** — One set at the top of each hour through the day; Grease the Groove on a clock.
- **Death By** — An EMOM that adds one rep each minute until you cannot finish the minute. *e.g.* Death by pull-ups
- **Deck of Cards** — Each card sets the reps (face value) and its suit picks the exercise; work through the deck.
- **Stappers** — As many rounds as possible of a short fixed circuit in 20 minutes, reps kept low enough to avoid failing. *e.g.* Mark Lauren's format
- **Max-rep test** — One all-out set or timed set, used to set or reset a program. *e.g.* One Hundred Push-ups initial test
- **Skill-practice block** — Fresh, high-quality practice of a technical skill, done first and stopped before form breaks down. *e.g.* 10 minutes of handstand practice
- **Complex & flow** — A fixed chain of movements done back to back without rest; flows stress smooth transitions. *e.g.* an Animal Flow sequence
- **Contrast pairing** — A hard strength version followed straight away by an explosive version of the same pattern. *e.g.* archer push-ups, then clap push-ups
- **Autoregulation** — End sets by effort, not a fixed count, such as stopping one or two reps short of failure.
- **Partner formats** — Alternate turns ("I go, you go"), split the work, or move in sync. *e.g.* Maxim 56
- **Training splits** — How exercises spread across the week: full body, upper/lower, push/pull/legs, or skill and strength days.

## Programs & routines

Established bodyweight training programs.

- **RCAF 5BX** *(Royal Canadian Air Force, 1961)* — 11 minutes a day, five exercises in order: toe-touch stretch (2 min), sit-up (1), back extension (1), push-up (1) and a stationary run with jumps (6). Six charts of 12 levels; each chart uses harder versions (knee push-ups up to chest-slap push-ups). Move up only when all five fit in 11 minutes; the target level is set by age.
- **RCAF XBX** *(Royal Canadian Air Force, 1960s)* — 12 minutes a day, ten exercises: toe touching, knee raising, lateral bending, arm circling, sit-ups, chest and leg raising, side leg raising, push-ups, leg-overs and running in place with jumps. Four charts, 48 levels, target set by age.
- **Convict Conditioning (the Big Six)** *(Paul Wade, 2009)* — Push-up, squat, pull-up, leg raise, bridge and handstand push-up, each a 10-step progression with rep standards for moving up; 2–4 warm-up sets, then 2–3 work sets. Sample programs: New Blood (2 days), Good Behavior (3 days), Veterano (one movement a day), Solitary Confinement (three pairs twice a week, plus grip, calves and neck) and Supermax (10–50 sets per movement). Convict Conditioning 2 (2011) adds grip, flags, calves and neck.
- **Recommended Routine** *(r/bodyweightfitness)* — 3 full-body sessions a week, about an hour. Warm-up, then three strength pairs at 3 × 5–8 with 90 s rest (pull-ups + squats, dips + hinges, rows + push-ups), then a core triplet (anti-extension, anti-rotation, extension) at 3 × 8–12. Tempo 10X0; stop a rep short of failure; move to the next progression at 3 × 8.
- **Minimalist Routine** *(r/bodyweightfitness)* — 2–6 rounds of walking lunges, push-ups, rows and plank shoulder taps with little rest; stop 1–2 reps short of failure; about 3 times a week.
- **One Hundred Push-ups** *(hundredpushups.com)* — Test your max, then 6 weeks of 3 sessions a week, 5–9 sets per session with the last set to the max. Columns are chosen by test results; the goal is 100 push-ups in a row. Sister plans cover sit-ups, squats, dips and pull-ups.
- **Armstrong Pull-up Program** *(Maj. Charles Lewis Armstrong, USMC)* — Three max sets of push-ups every morning. Pull-ups Monday to Friday: Day 1 five max sets; Day 2 a pyramid (1, 2, 3… to failure); Day 3 nine sets across three grips; Day 4 max training sets; Day 5 repeat the hardest day. Goal: 20 pull-ups in 6–8 weeks.
- **Fighter Pullup Program** *(Pavel Tsatsouline)* — A five-set ladder counting down from your max (5-4-3-2-1), adding one rep to one set each day; 5 days on, 1 off, for 30 days, then retest.
- **7-Minute Workout** *(Klika & Jordan, 2013)* — 12 exercises for 30 s each with 10 s transitions: jumping jacks, wall sit, push-up, crunch, step-up onto a chair, squat, triceps dip on a chair, plank, high knees, lunge, push-up with rotation, side plank. Repeat 2–3 times if you like.
- **You Are Your Own Gym** *(Mark Lauren with Joshua Clark)* — Four 10-week programs (Basic, 1st Class, Master Class, Chief Class), about 30 minutes four times a week alternating push/pull and legs/core days, using ladders, supersets, interval sets, timed sets, Tabatas and Stappers.
- **Original Tabata protocol** *(Tabata et al., 1996)* — 7–8 bouts of 20 s at about 170% of VO2max with 10 s rest, to exhaustion, on a braked exercise bike; 5 days a week for 6 weeks. Raised VO2max by about 7 ml/kg/min and anaerobic capacity by 28%. Bodyweight "Tabatas" borrow the timing, not the intensity.
- **Combat Conditioning ("Royal Court")** *(Matt Furey, 2000)* — Hindu squats, Hindu push-ups and the wrestler's bridge. Start squats at 25–50 reps with 500 in a row as a long-term goal; hold the wrestler's bridge for up to 3 minutes.
- **Pehlwani dands and baithaks** *(Indian wrestling tradition)* — Wrestlers train Hindu push-ups (dands) and Hindu squats (baithaks) in the hundreds or thousands; accounts of the Great Gama claim thousands of each daily. Many named dand and baithak variants exist.
- **Walter Camp's Daily Dozen** *(Walter Camp, 1919)* — 12 short mobility drills in four groups (Hands, Hips, Head; Grind, Grate, Grasp; Crawl, Curl, Crouch; Wave, Weave, Wing), about 15 minutes a day.
- **US Army PRT drills** *(US Army, TC 3-22.20 and ATP 7-22.02)* — Preparation Drill (bend and reach, rear lunge, high jumper, rower, squat bender, windmill, forward lunge, prone row, bent-leg body twist, push-up); Conditioning Drill 1 (power jump, V-up, mountain climber, leg tuck and twist, single-leg push-up); Conditioning Drill 2 (turn and lunge, supine bicycle, half jacks, swimmer, 8-count push-up); Conditioning Drill 3 (jumping and agility); 4 for the Core (bent-leg raise, side bridge, back bridge, quadraplex); Hip and Shoulder Stability Drills; Climbing Drills on a bar. 5–10 reps each.
